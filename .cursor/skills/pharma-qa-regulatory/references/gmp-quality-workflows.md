# GMP quality-system workflows

For complaint translation/content review or complaint-triggered reporting clocks, also read [complaint-translation-and-content-review.md](complaint-translation-and-content-review.md), targeted review 2026-10-08.

## Quality-system documents

Draft and review documents according to their intended function and the user's document hierarchy. Establish scope, definitions, responsibilities, sequence, controls, decision points, records, data-integrity expectations, deviations/exceptions, escalation, training, references, and lifecycle controls as applicable.

Use requirements that are specific enough to execute and audit. Do not invent site roles, system names, forms, timelines, alert/action limits, sampling plans, or approval authorities. Mark missing controlled information as a placeholder or question.

For review findings, provide: location; observed text or gap; requirement/evidence; quality or compliance impact; proposed revision; and any fact requiring confirmation.

## Investigation foundation

For deviations, laboratory events, complaints, failures, or trends:

1. Protect patient, product, data, and supply as needed; document immediate corrections or containment.
2. Define the event, expected state, actual state, time window, scope, batches/systems/markets, and discovery path.
3. Preserve records and data. Build a chronology from contemporaneous evidence.
4. Separate facts, hypotheses, and conclusions. Test plausible hypotheses rather than selecting a favored cause.
5. Assess impact on identity, strength, quality, purity, safety, efficacy, data integrity, validation state, and other potentially affected product or systems.
6. Determine root cause(s) and contributing factors at the appropriate system level, or document why a definitive cause was not established.
7. Define corrections, CAPA, owners, dates, effectiveness checks, residual risk, escalation, and recurrence monitoring.

Avoid unsupported “human error,” premature closure, retrospective reconstruction, and repeated testing intended to obtain a passing result.

## Topic-specific controls

| Deviation | Classification rationale, immediate action, scope, chronology, impact, investigation depth, recurrence, CAPA, closure evidence, and trending. |
| OOS | Preserve original data; perform scientifically sound laboratory assessment and, when not conclusively resolved, full-scale investigation; invalidate only with documented assignable cause; avoid testing into compliance. |
| OOT/trending | Distinguish an atypical trend from specification failure; verify statistical and scientific relevance, historical pattern, method/process variability, and potential emerging loss of control. |
| Root cause | Use evidence-based causal analysis; distinguish root, direct, and contributing causes; test causal statements against recurrence and scope. |
| CAPA | Link actions to causes and risks; separate correction, corrective action, and preventive/systemic action; define owner, due date, effectiveness method, acceptance criteria, and escalation. |
| Change control | Define rationale, scope, risk, impacted documents/systems/products/markets, regulatory assessment, validation/qualification, implementation prerequisites, training, effectiveness, and closure. |
| Supplier management | Apply risk-based qualification, quality agreements, material/service controls, performance monitoring, changes, deviations, audits, requalification, and disqualification/escalation. |
| MAH / holder lifecycle risk management | Separate statutory holder/applicant duties, product benefit-risk/PV controls, GMP/PQS quality risks, outsourced-activity risks, regulatory commitments, change risks, supply/availability risks, and market-action risks. Do not assume that China MAH, EU MAH, US applicant/application-holder, and PIC/S manufacturing-authorisation-holder concepts are legally equivalent. |
| PQR/APR | Define period, product/strength/site scope and any grouping rationale; reconcile the complete batch/data population; distinguish explicit jurisdictional minimum inputs from global-template enhancements; trend process, analytical, stability, deviations/OOS/OOT, changes, CAPA, complaints, returns/recalls, validation/qualification, suppliers/outsourcing and commitments as applicable; convert signals into controlled decisions and follow-up. Use [product-quality-review-regulatory-map.md](product-quality-review-regulatory-map.md) for China/EU/PIC/S/US/EAEU/ICH source routing and minimum-content differences. |
| Validation/qualification | Maintain lifecycle logic from requirements and risk assessment through protocol, predefined acceptance criteria, execution, deviations, report, release, change control, continued verification, and periodic review. | Intermediate/bulk hold time | Define the material/stage, clock start/stop, proposed maximum and routine hold, storage/container/transport conditions, relevant CQAs/processability risks, evidence route, actual-time recording, exceeded-hold handling, change/regulatory assessment, and lifecycle review. Do not impose a global 30-day rule or a universal batch count. |

| Complaint | Preserve the original allegation, receipt time, evidence, and traceability; triage quality defect, safety/pharmacovigilance, falsification, distribution, and regulatory-reporting branches in parallel; assess investigation need and extent across related batches/products/markets; apply risk controls, root-cause/CAPA, complainant response, trending, PQR/APR, and closure. For China/US/EU work, use [complaints-quality-defects-and-recalls.md](complaints-quality-defects-and-recalls.md). Do not wait for full root-cause completion before a separate time-critical reporting or recall trigger is met. |
| Recall | Assess health hazard, defect scope, affected distribution, and supply impact; define decision authority, regulatory communication, classification/depth, traceability, consignee/public communication, effectiveness checks, reconciliation, disposition, termination, CAPA, and closure. Use [recall-regulatory-map.md](recall-regulatory-map.md) for China/US/EU legal routes and currentness checkpoints and [recall-decision-framework.md](recall-decision-framework.md) for execution/closure design. | Self-inspection | Preserve independence and competence; plan risk-based scope; collect objective evidence; classify findings using defined criteria; assign CAPA; verify effectiveness; trend systemic weaknesses. |


## Product quality review / annual quality evaluation workflow

For a PQR/APR task, first determine whether the subject is a finished medicinal product or an API and identify the target market(s). Then use [product-quality-review-regulatory-map.md](product-quality-review-regulatory-map.md) to separate the applicable legal/GMP minimum from the company global template. In particular:

1. distinguish China PQR, EU/PIC/S/EAEU PQR, US 21 CFR 211.180(e) annual evaluation, and ICH Q7 API PQR rather than treating them as interchangeable;
2. define the review period, product/strength/site population and any rolling-review or grouping rationale;
3. reconcile the batch census and source data before trending, including rejected or otherwise relevant batches and missing/late data;
4. map each review input to its regulatory source or label it as professional/internal enhancement;
5. evaluate recurrence, adverse trends, state of control, specification suitability, validation/qualification status, registered-state consistency, outsourced-activity performance and prior actions as applicable;
6. convert conclusions into CAPA, change control, requalification/revalidation, enhanced monitoring, supplier/contractor action or regulatory assessment where justified; and
7. verify ownership, approval, due dates, effectiveness and carry-over into the next review or management review.

Do not invent statistical alert/action limits. Do not state that a US APR must contain the EU/PIC/S twelve-item list merely because a company elects to use one global template. Treat current EU/PIC/S revised Chapter 1 material as draft until it is adopted and effective.


## PIC/S PE 009-18 reference and retention sample workflow

For a PIC/S reference/retention-sample task, first confirm whether the question concerns ordinary finished-product sampling, starting/packaging material samples, intermediates, or the special parallel imported/distributed/traded repackaging route. Then use [pics-pe009-18-regulatory-map.md](pics-pe009-18-regulatory-map.md) and the controlled Annex 19 copy.

For revised Annex 19 Section 9, explicitly determine whether applicable national law covers the parallel-import/distribution/trade scenario. If applicable, map physical packaging-material samples, reference-sample applicability, one-retention-sample-per-repackaging-operation, retention duration, sample representativeness, and any proposed photographic/digital exception. A digital route requires documented justification and prior competent-authority agreement; electronic records should be controlled using Annex 11 principles.

When samples support a complaint, suspected quality defect or recall investigation, preserve sample traceability and link the evidence to the relevant complaint/recall workflow.

## Intermediate and bulk hold-time workflow

For intermediate or bulk product hold-time work, first identify whether the material is a finished-dosage-form intermediate/bulk product, sterile bulk, API intermediate, or another special category. Define the exact manufacturing stage and the objective timestamp events that start and stop the hold clock. Then:

1. map each target market and the applicable source hierarchy using [intermediate-product-hold-time-regulatory-map.md](intermediate-product-hold-time-regulatory-map.md);
2. determine whether the source calls for a dedicated study, permits a risk/science-based justification, expects dossier data, or treats the matter primarily as GMP validation;
3. assess chemical, physical, microbiological, processability, container/headspace, environmental, transport, and cumulative-hold risks;
4. select batches, scale, study duration, time points, attributes, container/closure, and conditions from the governing source plus scientific risk;
5. challenge the intended maximum hold or representative worst case and link intermediate findings to downstream processing and finished-product CQAs;
6. record actual elapsed times in routine batch documentation and investigate any exceeded approved limit;
7. route hold-time or storage-condition extensions through change control and market-specific regulatory assessment; and
8. review trends, recurrent exceeded holds, new stability/process knowledge, transfers, and relevant process changes for re-study or reconfirmation triggers.

For practical design and review templates, use [intermediate-product-hold-time-study-framework.md](intermediate-product-hold-time-study-framework.md). Do not justify an exceeded hold solely by retesting a sample into compliance, and do not assume PPQ validates a hold time that was not actually challenged or otherwise supported.

## Dedicated recall governance

For a recall-specific task, identify first whether the action is a true recall or another market action and which legal actor owns the decision. Use [recall-regulatory-map.md](recall-regulatory-map.md) before assigning a classification, deadline, authority route, or termination criterion. Then use [recall-decision-framework.md](recall-decision-framework.md) for the operational sequence.

Keep four closure states separate: recall execution complete; regulatory recall terminated/closed; quality investigation complete; and CAPA/change effectiveness complete. A recall can finish operationally before CAPA effectiveness is complete, and a CAPA plan does not by itself terminate a recall.

For the United States, distinguish recall, correction, market withdrawal, and stock recovery under 21 CFR 7.3 and remember that FDA assigns formal recall Class I/II/III. For China, use the NMPA Level I/II/III framework and its current statutory clocks. For the EU, distinguish MAH/manufacturer recall, authority-ordered withdrawal/prohibition, quality-defect reporting, and Rapid Alert Network actions; confirm centrally authorised versus nationally authorised routing.

## Holder-level lifecycle risk governance

When a task concerns the MAH, applicant/application holder, product owner, or a manufacturing-authorisation holder rather than only a site-level GMP risk assessment, first identify the legal actor and governing market. Then map obligations across product safety/benefit-risk, pharmaceutical quality, outsourcing, changes, post-marketing commitments, complaints/quality defects/recalls, supply continuity where applicable, and periodic management review.

Use [mah-holder-risk-management-regulatory-map.md](mah-holder-risk-management-regulatory-map.md) for China, EU, US, and PIC/S clause routing. Do not copy EU RMP terminology into the US or PIC/S unless the applicable product or authority actually requires that construct. Likewise, do not treat an ICH Q9 assessment as satisfying a separate statutory MAH pharmacovigilance or post-marketing risk-plan duty.

## China MAH, QP, release, and contract manufacturing

For China-market work, determine whether the actor is the MAH, a drug manufacturer, a contract manufacturer, or more than one of these. Do not transfer the MAH's statutory quality-and-safety responsibility through a contract or quality agreement.

- Distinguish factory release from marketing release. Confirm the governing procedure, release authority, quality agreement, and records; do not use the two terms interchangeably.
- Render `质量受权人` as `Qualified Person (QP)`. Do not import the EU QP certification model into a China analysis unless comparing the systems explicitly.
- For batch documents, use `batch review and release` for `审核放行`; reserve `release review` for the review step before the final release decision.
- In contract manufacturing, assess both the MAH's lifecycle oversight and the contract manufacturer's GMP duties, including technical transfer, approved process, quality agreement, deviation/change communication, audit access, batch information, release interfaces, and ongoing performance monitoring.
- Apply NMPA Announcement No. 134 of 2025 together with—not as an assumed wholesale replacement for—Announcement No. 132 of 2023 and other cited legal bases. Verify product-specific and transition provisions before making a conclusion.
- Treat provincial QP or contract-manufacturing rules as regional requirements. Do not generalize a Guangdong requirement nationwide without a national basis.

## API change control and ICH Q7

For API work, inspect the current ICH Q7 text, relevant Q&A, and applicable regional implementation before quoting or assigning clause numbers. A sound change-control workflow normally includes a formal proposal, scientific and quality-risk assessment, Quality Unit review or approval as applicable, regulatory and customer impact assessment, validation or qualification, document and training updates, authorized implementation, effectiveness review, and closure. Label this workflow as professional synthesis unless each element is tied to an inspected requirement.

Evaluate potential impact on API quality, registered or filed information, downstream dosage-form manufacture, stability, validation state, specifications or analytical methods, suppliers and materials, computerized systems, and affected customers or contract parties. Do not implement first and reconstruct the assessment later except under a documented emergency process permitted by the governing system.

## Data integrity and evidence

Apply attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original/true-copy, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and available data principles as appropriate. Review metadata, audit trails, access controls, interfaces, calculations, exclusions, reprocessing, and record retention when relevant.

Do not treat a signature, checklist, or procedural statement as proof that an activity occurred. Distinguish designed controls from implementation evidence and effectiveness evidence.

## Professional judgment boundaries

Recommend proportionate actions based on product, patient, process, detection, recurrence, and system risk. Clearly label recommendations that go beyond explicit regulatory text. Confirm site procedures and jurisdiction-specific timelines before presenting them as mandatory.
