# ICH Q9(R1) interface with other ICH Quality Guidelines

## Purpose

Use this file after [quality-risk-management.md](quality-risk-management.md) when QRM must be integrated with pharmaceutical development, PQS, API development/manufacture, lifecycle change, continuous manufacturing or analytical procedure development.

ICH's Q9(R1) Step 4 presentation describes Q9(R1) as a **foundational guideline** supporting implementation of Q7, Q8, Q10, Q11, Q12, Q13 and other Quality Guidelines. Treat that statement as a routing principle, not as permission to merge the legal or technical requirements of separate guidelines.

Current ICH Quality Guidelines index: `https://admin.ich.org/page/quality-guidelines`

## Interface map

| Guideline | Primary subject | Q9(R1) interface | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Q7** | GMP for active pharmaceutical ingredients | Use QRM to support risk-based GMP decisions, investigations, supplier/outsourced controls, validation/qualification and lifecycle control where appropriate. | QRM does not waive explicit Q7/GMP expectations. Verify regional implementation and the applicable API/intermediate scope. |
| **Q8(R2)** | Pharmaceutical development | Use QRM with product/process knowledge to identify/prioritise CQAs, material attributes, CPPs, development experiments and control-strategy risks. | Risk ranking is not proof of criticality by itself and does not replace development data or scientific rationale. |
| **Q10** | Pharmaceutical Quality System | Q10 knowledge management and PQS provide the governance/knowledge environment for QRM. Q9(R1) specifically cross-refers to Q10 for knowledge management and enhanced monitoring of outsourced activities/suppliers. | Do not treat QRM as a stand-alone department or document system. Embed decisions, review and feedback in the PQS. |
| **Q11** | Development and manufacture of drug substances | Apply QRM to drug-substance process understanding, material attributes, process parameters, control strategy, scale-up and lifecycle knowledge. | Keep development risk assessment distinct from GMP compliance and dossier commitments. |
| **Q12** | Pharmaceutical product lifecycle management | Use QRM and process/product knowledge to support post-approval change assessment, established conditions strategy, PACMP and PQS change management. | QRM cannot replace regional post-approval change reporting/approval categories. Q12 implementation is region-dependent. |
| **Q13** | Continuous manufacturing | Apply QRM to material flow, state of control, disturbances, diversion, control strategy, equipment/system integration and lifecycle changes. | Do not assume continuous manufacturing is inherently lower risk; evaluate technology-specific failure pathways and control performance. |
| **Q14** | Analytical procedure development | Q14 explicitly encourages QRM to identify/prioritise analytical procedure parameters, assess performance risks, guide experiments and inform ongoing monitoring/risk review. | Risk assessment can be formal or informal but must evolve with knowledge. Pair with Q2(R2) for validation when the task concerns analytical validation. |
| **Q2(R2)** | Validation of analytical procedures | Use QRM/Q14 knowledge to define what performance characteristics and evidence are important for the analytical procedure and its intended use. | QRM supports validation strategy; it does not waive validation expectations or acceptance criteria required by the procedure's intended purpose. |

## Q9(R1) - Q10 linkage

Give special attention to the Q9/Q10 interface because weak knowledge management is a recurring root cause of weak risk decisions.

Use Q10 knowledge-management concepts to:
- identify and retrieve relevant development, validation, change, deviation, stability, CPV, supplier and complaint knowledge;
- reduce uncertainty before consequential decisions;
- prevent legacy scores or undocumented expert opinion from becoming the only evidence base;
- feed new knowledge back into risk review;
- maintain decision rationale across site transfer, outsourcing and personnel changes.

The Q9(R1) Step 4 presentation highlights four Q10 cross-references, including use of relevant data/knowledge to control subjectivity, knowledge management for decision-making, and enhanced monitoring of outsourced activities/suppliers when substantial variability is identified.

## Q8/Q9/Q10 implementation support

When the task involves integrated Q8/Q9/Q10 implementation, also check the current ICH/FDA Q8-Q9-Q10 Q&A route. The US FDA issued **Q8, Q9, and Q10 Questions and Answers (R5)** as Final Level 2 Guidance in May 2026. Distinguish the ICH source/version from the regional FDA implementation status.

FDA checkpoint: `https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q8-q9-and-q10-questions-and-answers-r5`

## Analytical lifecycle rule - Q14

For analytical procedure development and lifecycle work:
1. use prior knowledge to frame the analytical target and plausible performance risks;
2. use QRM early to identify parameters/steps that may affect performance;
3. update the assessment as development evidence grows;
4. use risk review to inform ongoing monitoring and continual improvement;
5. document the QRM outcome in the relevant PQS/dossier context;
6. pair Q14 with Q2(R2), stability/compendial requirements and jurisdictional filing rules as applicable.

Current Q14 Step 4 text checked in 2026 includes a corrected ICH-hosted version and explicitly contains a section on `Knowledge and Risk Management in Analytical Procedure Development and Continual Improvement`.

## Lifecycle-change rule - Q12

For post-approval change questions, first determine the regional regulatory category. Use Q9 to assess science/risk and Q10 to assess PQS/change-system effectiveness, then use Q12 and the jurisdictional framework to determine regulatory strategy. Never conclude that a low QRM score authorises implementation when a prior approval or notification route applies.

## Output rule

When multiple ICH guidelines apply, identify which guideline provides:
- the QRM principle or method;
- the product/process knowledge expectation;
- the PQS/governance expectation;
- the technical development/validation expectation;
- the lifecycle regulatory tool;
- the regional legal implementation route.

Do not collapse all of these into a generic statement that `ICH requires` a company-specific practice.
