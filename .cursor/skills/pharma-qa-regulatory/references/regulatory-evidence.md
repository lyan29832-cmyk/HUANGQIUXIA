# Regulatory research and evidence

## Source hierarchy

Prioritize sources in this order while preserving their actual legal status:

1. Binding law, regulation, pharmacopoeial requirement, or formally adopted text on the competent authority's official site.
2. Current official GMP text, annex, guidance, Q&A, enforcement policy, inspection manual, or regulator communication.
3. Formally adopted ICH guideline or recognized standard, with implementation status stated for the jurisdiction.
4. User-provided regulatory files, cited with their exact version and treated as controlled references rather than automatically current.
5. Reliable secondary literature used only for context or discovery, never as the sole support for a decisive regulatory claim when a primary source is available.

Do not imply that ICH text is independently binding without considering regional adoption. Do not imply that PIC/S guidance is automatically domestic law. For FDA work, distinguish binding requirements such as applicable CFR provisions from nonbinding guidance. For EU and China work, state the status and scope of the specific instrument rather than relying on the label “GMP.”

## Currentness and validity verification

For a request involving `latest`, `current`, `effective`, `valid`, `withdrawn`, `superseded`, or equivalent wording:

1. Search the competent authority's official website or official register.
2. Open the actual source page or official document; do not rely on a search-result snippet.
3. Check revision history, effective/application dates, corrigenda, replacement notices, and scope.
4. Cross-check the authority's current index or consolidated page when available.
5. Record the verification date and official URL.
6. If status remains uncertain, state precisely what was and was not verified.

Use the current date at execution time; never rely on the Skill creation date. A bundled or uploaded copy may support text analysis or historical comparison but cannot by itself establish current status.

Before searching, read [regulatory-source-register.md](regulatory-source-register.md) for the known source family, historical filenames, and version traps. The register is not proof of currentness; its date is a checkpoint. An official source inspected later controls.

Assign one explicit status to every source whose currency matters:

- `effective/current for the stated scope`;
- `historical/superseded`;
- `withdrawn/repealed`;
- `published but not yet effective/applicable`;
- `draft/consultation/concept paper`;
- `status not verified`.

Do not treat publication as effectiveness. Compare publication, entry-into-force, transition, and full-application dates. Do not treat a newer document on a related topic as replacing an older document unless a repeal, replacement, or supersession statement—or a legally sound reconciliation—supports that conclusion.

## Controlled copies and current official sources

- Use the user's controlled copy to establish the wording of that exact version and the official source to establish current status.
- If the controlled copy and official page differ, identify the difference and determine whether it is translation, corrigendum, consolidation, revision, or scope—not merely “conflict.”
- A translated official page may aid comprehension, but identify the controlling language when it affects legal meaning.
- For ICH, record both the guideline version and the relevant jurisdiction's adoption or implementation status.
- For PIC/S, distinguish the PIC/S GMP Guide version from the domestic law or regulatory basis under which an authority applies it.
- For EAEU work, distinguish EEC acts and common procedures from Member State law, inspectorate administration, and project-specific requests; read [eaeu-regulatory-and-gmp.md](eaeu-regulatory-and-gmp.md).
- For EU work, use the current EudraLex Volume 4 index and the applicable human or veterinary framework; do not assume one index governs both after their regulatory paths diverge.
- For FDA work, verify binding text in the current eCFR and distinguish it from FDA guidance, compliance programs, warning letters, and other nonbinding materials.

## Evidence record

For each material regulatory proposition, capture as available:

- issuing authority and jurisdiction;
- exact title and identifier;
- part, chapter, annex, section, clause, or page;
- publication, revision, adoption, effective, or application date;
- status and scope;
- a short faithful paraphrase or a minimal quotation;
- official URL and access date.

Place citations near the claims they support. Never cite a clause that was not inspected. If relying on a translation of an official text, identify the authoritative-language issue when material.

## Separate statement types

Label or clearly distinguish:

- `Regulatory text`: what the inspected source expressly states.
- `Regulatory interpretation`: a reasoned reading of how the text applies to the defined facts.
- `Professional assessment`: a risk-based recommendation, industry practice, implementation option, or predicted inspection concern.

Avoid phrases such as “the regulator requires” for the latter two categories. State assumptions and alternative reasonable interpretations.

## Clause interpretation workflow

1. Quote or accurately paraphrase only the necessary text.
2. Establish definitions, scope, exceptions, cross-references, and legal or guidance status.
3. Read surrounding clauses and relevant annexes/Q&As; avoid interpreting a sentence in isolation.
4. Map the text to the user's facts without importing unstated facts.
5. Explain ambiguity and the practical consequence of each plausible interpretation.
6. Provide a professional recommendation separately from the regulatory conclusion.

## Cross-jurisdiction comparison

Compare by substantive topic, not section numbering. Use columns such as:

| Topic | China/NMPA | EAEU | EU | PIC/S | US FDA | ICH | Common requirement | Material difference | Potential compliance impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include only applicable columns. For each row:

- normalize scope and terminology before comparing;
- distinguish binding requirements, adopted guidance, and harmonizing principles;
- identify common intent separately from identical wording;
- describe a difference only after checking context, exceptions, and effective versions;
- explain the operational or quality-system impact without overstating enforcement certainty;
- write `not identified in the reviewed sources` when evidence is insufficient to claim absence.

Conclude with: common requirements; material differences; possible compliance impacts; unresolved evidence gaps; and the source/version basis of the comparison.
