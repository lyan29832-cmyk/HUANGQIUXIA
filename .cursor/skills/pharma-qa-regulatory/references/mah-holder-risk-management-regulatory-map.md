# MAH / holder lifecycle risk-management regulatory map

## Purpose

Use this reference when the task concerns lifecycle risk governance by a marketing authorisation holder (MAH), applicant/application holder, product owner, or a manufacturer acting under a holder relationship in China, the European Union, the United States, or a PIC/S GMP environment.

This reference was specifically currentness-checked on **2026-09-18**. Reopen the current official source before relying on exact wording, legal status, applicability, reporting timelines, or a jurisdiction-specific conclusion.

Do not use this file to imply that the four systems are legally equivalent. They are not.

## Core terminology and legal-boundary rules

1. **China:** use `Drug Marketing Authorization Holder (MAH)` for the statutory holder under the Drug Administration Law. China imposes explicit lifecycle, post-marketing risk-management-plan, pharmacovigilance, outsourcing-oversight, annual-reporting, and recurring risk-review duties on the MAH.
2. **European Union:** use `Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH)`. Distinguish two linked but different risk domains:
   - the legally defined pharmacovigilance `risk management system` and product-specific `Risk Management Plan (RMP)`; and
   - GMP-related quality-risk, PQR, outsourcing, quality-defect, recall, and MA-compliance responsibilities.
3. **United States:** do not automatically translate the China/EU MAH concept into `MAH`. Determine the regulated role first: `applicant`, `NDA/ANDA holder`, `application holder`, `licensed manufacturer`, or `manufacturer`. There is **no single universal US post-marketing RMP regime equivalent to the China MAH risk-management-plan duty or the EU RMP system for all products**. Build the US route from applicable statutes/regulations plus CGMP and FDA guidance. REMS is product-specific and applies only when FDA requires it.
4. **PIC/S:** do not describe PIC/S as creating an independent MAH legal regime. PIC/S PE 009 is a GMP guide used by participating authorities through their own legal/administrative frameworks. It contains important references to the Marketing Authorisation holder, manufacturer, Contract Giver, QRM, PQR, quality defects, and recalls, but legal enforceability comes through the relevant participating authority's implementation. PIC/S PI documents are inspection guidance/recommendations, not substitutes for PE 009 or national law.
5. Keep `product safety / benefit-risk management` separate from `pharmaceutical quality risk management (QRM)`. They interact, but one must not be presented as a substitute for the other.

## China - MAH risk-management requirements

### A. Drug Administration Law of the People's Republic of China (2019 revision)

Treat the following as the statutory backbone:

- **Article 30:** the MAH is responsible for nonclinical studies, clinical trials, production and distribution, post-marketing research, adverse-reaction monitoring/reporting/handling and related activities; the legal representative/principal bears overall responsibility for drug quality.
- **Article 31:** the MAH shall establish a quality-assurance system, designate independent quality-management personnel, periodically audit entrusted manufacturers and distributors, and supervise their continuing quality-assurance/control capability.
- **Article 35:** where storage or transportation is outsourced, assess the contractor's quality-assurance and risk-management capability, establish agreements, and supervise performance.
- **Article 37:** the MAH annual report includes production/sales, post-marketing studies and risk management.
- **Article 77:** the MAH shall establish a post-marketing risk-management plan, proactively conduct post-marketing research, further confirm safety, efficacy and quality controllability, and strengthen continuing management of marketed drugs.
- **Article 78:** conditionally approved drugs require corresponding risk-management measures and completion of required studies.
- **Article 79:** manufacturing changes are classified by risk/impact; the MAH shall comprehensively assess and verify the impact on safety, efficacy and quality controllability.
- **Articles 80-83:** post-marketing ADR monitoring, risk-control action for identified risks, quality/safety-hazard recall duties, and periodic post-marketing evaluation are part of the lifecycle risk framework.
- **Article 127:** failure to establish the required post-marketing risk-management plan, conduct required post-marketing research/evaluation, submit annual reports, or make required change filings can trigger legal consequences.

Official checkpoint: `https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_b73ec9dabb2d468281d6c994b4bcdaf8.html`

### B. Regulations for Implementation of the Drug Administration Law, State Council Decree No. 828 (2026 revision)

Current version: issued 2026-01-16 and effective 2026-05-15.

- **Article 23:** MAH must establish a sound drug quality-assurance system, independent quality department, and required key quality/manufacturing roles.
- **Article 24:** MAH must comply with GVP, maintain a pharmacovigilance system, and establish mechanisms for risk identification, assessment and control.
- **Article 25:** the designated domestic legal entity for an overseas MAH must have corresponding quality-management and risk-control capability.
- **Article 27:** MAH must comprehensively assess and verify the impact of manufacturing-process changes on safety, efficacy and quality controllability. Regulators may order correction/risk controls where evidence is insufficient or the classification is inappropriate.
- **Article 28:** MAH must continuously examine quality, efficacy and ADRs; conduct periodic post-marketing evaluation and integrated benefit-risk assessment; and take risk-control measures such as labeling revision, higher quality standards, process improvement, suspension of production/sale, recall, or cancellation where warranted.
- **Article 31:** for contract manufacture, the MAH retains supplier audit, manufacturing change management, market release and oversight responsibilities; segmented manufacturing requires one unified quality-assurance system covering the entire process and all sites.
- **Article 36:** where specified pre-approval commercial-scale batches are marketed after approval, the MAH must strengthen risk management of those batches.

Official checkpoint: `https://www.mee.gov.cn/zcwj/gwywj/202601/t20260128_1142757.shtml`

### C. Provisions for the Supervision and Administration of Drug Manufacturing, SAMR Order No. 28

- **Article 27-28:** MAH establishes a quality-assurance system, audits contracted manufacturers/distributors, and must respond to major quality-safety events according to its risk-management plan.
- **Article 31:** MAH/manufacturer risk management includes risk assessment, control, communication and review throughout manufacturing.
- **Article 39:** annual report includes risk management.
- **Article 40:** MAH continuously conducts drug benefit-risk assessment/control, establishes a post-marketing risk-management plan and actively conducts post-marketing studies.
- **Article 41:** MAH establishes the pharmacovigilance system.
- **Article 42:** before contract manufacture, MAH assesses the contractor's quality-assurance and risk-management capability and supervises the contractor.
- **Article 44:** annual product quality review supports continuing product/process risk understanding; specified commercial-scale batches marketed after approval require strengthened risk management.
- **Article 53:** inspection scope expressly includes implementation of the risk-management plan and change management.

Official checkpoint: `https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_65070d0ee03a4109ac831ee7b3cee51c.html`

### D. NMPA Announcement No. 126 of 2022 - MAH primary responsibility for drug quality and safety

Use this document as the most practical China MAH governance layer:

- **Articles 3, 11-12:** establish lifecycle and manufacturing quality-management systems and continuously improve them.
- **Article 14:** establish post-marketing change control, internal classification, procedures and risk-management requirements.
- **Articles 16-17:** evaluate contract-manufacturing, storage, transportation and sales contractors for quality-assurance and risk-management capability; audit and supervise them. Quality agreements cannot transfer statutory MAH responsibility.
- **Articles 19-22:** maintain recall and PV systems; establish a post-marketing risk-management plan; periodically evaluate benefit-risk; and implement quality-improvement/risk-control measures. Maintain an emergency response plan for major quality-safety events.
- **Article 24:** maintain compensation capability proportionate to product risk, market scale and potential injury.
- **Article 25:** investigate deviations and control potential quality risks.
- **Article 26:** the Quality Head shall organise periodic review based on product risk; as a general rule, recurring and emerging risks are reviewed at least quarterly. The company head must hear quality-risk reports and provide resources for risk control.
- **Article 27:** annual report includes risk management.
- **Article 28:** periodic self-inspection/internal audit checks GMP/GSP/GVP implementation and CAPA needs.

Official NMPA-origin checkpoint: `https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/xxgk/ggtg/qtggtg/20221229195805180.html`

### China interpretation rule

Do **not** reduce the China statutory `post-marketing risk-management plan` to a pharmacovigilance-only RMP. China law frames marketed-drug risk management more broadly around **safety, efficacy and quality controllability**, with interfaces to PV, quality systems, changes, outsourcing, post-marketing studies, annual reporting, recall and lifecycle evaluation. A company may have separate controlled documents for quality-risk management and PV risk-management activities, but the MAH must be able to demonstrate one coherent lifecycle governance model.

## European Union - MAH risk-management requirements

### A. Directive 2001/83/EC - pharmacovigilance risk-management system

Core MAH duties include:

- **Article 23:** after authorisation, keep manufacturing/control methods aligned with scientific and technical progress and promptly provide new information that can affect the marketing authorisation or evaluation of benefits and risks.
- **Article 104(1)-(3):** operate a pharmacovigilance system; scientifically evaluate information; consider risk-minimisation/prevention options; audit the PV system; maintain the PSMF; operate a risk-management system for each medicinal product subject to the legal transitional provisions; monitor the outcome of risk-minimisation measures; update the risk-management system; and monitor PV data for new/changed risks or benefit-risk changes.
- **Article 104a:** competent authorities may impose an RMS/RMP obligation on certain older products where concerns affect the benefit-risk balance.

Current consolidated Directive checkpoint: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02001L0083-20250101`

### B. Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 - centrally authorised products

- **Article 14a:** conditions imposed on an authorisation are incorporated into the MAH's risk-management system.
- **Article 21:** the MAH obligations in Directive 2001/83/EC Article 104 apply to centrally authorised human medicinal products. EMA may impose a risk-management-system obligation where concerns affect benefit-risk.

Official checkpoint: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2004/726/2022-01-28/eng`

### C. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 520/2012, as amended

For the legally specified RMP structure and maintenance, use the current consolidated version. Key provisions:

- **Article 30:** RMP content includes safety-profile identification/characterisation, further characterisation plans, risk prevention/minimisation measures and effectiveness assessment, and post-authorisation obligations.
- **Article 31:** RMP summary requirements.
- **Article 32:** MAH submission of updated RMPs; each version must be version-controlled and dated.
- **Article 33:** RMP format.

Current consolidated checkpoint (12 Feb 2026): `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2012/520/2026-02-12/eng`

### D. EMA GVP Module V and current RMP procedural guidance

Operational expectations include:

- all new marketing-authorisation applications include an RMP describing the RMS and its summary, subject to proportionality and product/legal-basis specifics;
- the MAH/applicant maintains an appropriate RMS, critically reviews the evolving safety profile, updates the RMS/RMP as needed, and monitors whether new risks arise, known risks change, or benefit-risk changes;
- RMPs are lifecycle documents and may need updating at authority request, after significant new information, when risk-minimisation arrangements change, or with significant changes to the marketing authorisation.

Current EMA route: `https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/marketing-authorisation/pharmacovigilance-marketing-authorisation/risk-management/risk-management-plans`

### E. EU GMP / MAH quality-risk interface

Do not treat EU RMP as the only MAH risk-management mechanism. GMP-related responsibilities remain separate and complementary.

Use EudraLex Volume 4 Part I, especially:

- **Chapter 1:** Pharmaceutical Quality System, Product Quality Review and Quality Risk Management. Where MAH and manufacturer differ, the MAH is expressly involved in evaluation of the PQR and relevant technical arrangements.
- **Chapter 7:** outsourced activities must be defined, agreed and controlled; MAH/manufacturer interfaces must be appropriately arranged.
- **Chapter 8:** complaints, quality defects and recalls use QRM for investigation extent, risk-reducing actions, CAPA and authority/MAH communication.
- **EMA Reflection Paper on GMP and Marketing Authorisation Holders, Version 2 (2022):** consolidates GMP-related MAH responsibilities and makes clear that tasks may be delegated but MAH responsibilities identified by the framework are not simply contracted away.

Current EU GMP index: `https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/eudralex/eudralex-volume-4_en`

EMA adopted reflection-paper route: `https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/reflection-paper-good-manufacturing-practice-marketing-authorisation-holders_en.pdf`

### EU interpretation rule

Keep **EU PV RMS/RMP** and **EU GMP quality-risk governance** as two interoperable systems. The former is a medicinal-product safety/benefit-risk framework; the latter governs pharmaceutical quality and manufacturing/distribution risks. Link them when a quality issue creates or changes a safety concern, when risk-minimisation action affects manufacture/supply, or when product-quality information changes the benefit-risk balance.

## United States - role-based risk-management requirements

### A. Do not invent a universal US MAH/RMP obligation

For a US task, first identify whether the regulated entity is the NDA/ANDA `applicant/application holder`, BLA `licensed manufacturer/application holder`, or a `manufacturer` subject to CGMP. Do not state that every US application holder must maintain an EU-style RMP. Instead, build the applicable controls from the following legal layers.

### B. Postmarketing safety information - 21 CFR 314.80 and 600.80

For approved drugs under 21 CFR 314.80:

- the applicant must promptly review adverse-drug-experience information obtained from domestic and foreign sources;
- written procedures are required for surveillance, receipt, evaluation and reporting;
- serious and unexpected adverse experiences have expedited reporting requirements;
- the applicant must investigate follow-up information and maintain required records;
- periodic reporting includes analysis and actions taken because of adverse experiences.

For biologics, use **21 CFR 600.80** for the corresponding postmarketing adverse-experience reporting framework.

Official checkpoints:
- `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-314/subpart-B/section-314.80`
- `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-600/subpart-D/section-600.80`

### C. Other postmarketing information and quality signals - 21 CFR 314.81

- **314.81(b)(1):** qualifying information about distributed NDA products triggers Field Alert Report assessment; the rule specifies a 3-working-day reporting clock from applicant receipt.
- **314.81(b)(2):** annual reports include significant new information that may affect safety, effectiveness or labeling and actions taken/intended.

Official checkpoint: `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-314/subpart-B/section-314.81`

### D. Lifecycle change risk - 21 CFR 314.70 and 601.12

- **21 CFR 314.70:** the NDA holder must assess the effects of a manufacturing change before distributing product made with that change. Reporting category is linked to the change's potential adverse effect on identity, strength, quality, purity or potency as related to safety/effectiveness.
- **21 CFR 601.12:** apply the BLA postapproval-change framework for licensed biological products.

These are regulatory change-classification/reporting rules. QRM can support the scientific assessment but cannot replace the required regulatory route.

Official checkpoints:
- `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-314/subpart-B/section-314.70`
- `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-601/subpart-C/section-601.12`

### E. CGMP quality risk and quality-system layer

Use enforceable CGMP requirements in **21 CFR Parts 210 and 211** together with FDA guidance where helpful. Important links include:

- 21 CFR 211.192 investigations and extension to other potentially affected batches/products;
- 21 CFR 211.198 complaint files;
- 21 CFR 211.180(e) annual product-quality evaluation / review inputs;
- FDA/ICH **Q9(R1) Quality Risk Management** (Final, May 2023);
- FDA/ICH **Q10 Pharmaceutical Quality System**;
- FDA **Quality Systems Approach to Pharmaceutical CGMP Regulations**;
- FDA/ICH **Q8, Q9, and Q10 Questions and Answers (R5)** (Final Level 2, May 2026).

FDA guidance documents do **not** create legally enforceable responsibilities unless they cite an applicable statute/regulation. Use them as current Agency/ICH implementation guidance, not as if they were CFR text.

Official FDA QRM checkpoint: `https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q9r1-quality-risk-management`

Official R5 checkpoint: `https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q8-q9-and-q10-questions-and-answers-r5`

### F. REMS - only when applicable

Under FD&C Act section 505-1, FDA can require a **Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS)** for certain prescription drugs/biologics when needed to ensure benefits outweigh serious risks. REMS is not a universal post-marketing RMP for every product.

When REMS applies, assess:
- REMS goals and required risk-minimisation elements;
- responsibilities of the application holder and other participants;
- implementation, assessment, modifications/revisions, and reporting obligations;
- interaction with labeling, postmarketing safety signals and benefit-risk reassessment.

Current FDA REMS route: `https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/risk-evaluation-and-mitigation-strategies-rems`

Statutory-factor guidance: `https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/rems-fdas-application-statutory-factors-determining-when-rems-necessary-guidance-industry`

### US interpretation rule

Do not write `US RMP` as a generic mandatory equivalent of the China MAH risk-management plan or EU RMP. Instead, present a **role-based US risk-control architecture**: CGMP/PQS/QRM + postmarketing safety reporting + application annual/FAR reporting + postapproval change control + recall/quality-defect controls + REMS when specifically required.

## PIC/S - GMP/QRM and MAH/manufacturer interfaces

### A. Current source status

At the 2026-09-24 checkpoint, the PIC/S publications index lists **PE 009-18** as the current GMP Guide, effective 24 September 2026. The PE 009-18 revision reason is Annex 19; Part I Chapter 1 was not substantively revised by this version change. Any separately published draft Chapter 1 remains draft and must not be substituted for the effective PE 009-18 Part I text.

Official publications index: `https://picscheme.org/en/publications`

### B. PE 009-18 Part I - current GMP clauses most relevant to holder risk governance

- **Chapter 1 / PQS:** senior management is responsible for an effective Pharmaceutical Quality System; GMP and QRM are integrated into the PQS.
- **1.10 Product Quality Review:** review includes changes, variations, stability/adverse trends, quality-related returns/complaints/recalls, prior CAPA, post-marketing commitments, qualification status and contractual arrangements.
- **1.11:** manufacturer and, where different, MAH should evaluate PQR results and assess need for CAPA or revalidation. Where MAH differs from manufacturer, a technical agreement defines responsibilities for PQR; the Authorised Person and MAH ensure timely/accurate review.
- **1.12-1.13 Quality Risk Management:** QRM is a systematic process for assessment, control, communication and review of medicinal-product quality risks; evaluation is science/experience based, linked ultimately to patient protection, and the effort/formality/documentation is commensurate with risk.
- **Chapter 7 (7.2-7.5):** outsourced arrangements must comply with regulations and the MA; appropriate arrangements are required where MAH and manufacturer differ. The Contract Giver PQS controls/reviews outsourcing using QRM and assesses legality, suitability, competence and performance.
- **Chapter 8 (8.10-8.15):** quality defects are assessed using QRM, with risk-proportionate investigation/actions; related batches/products and recurring signals are considered; risk-reducing actions are taken without waiting for perfect information; potentially recall/supply-restricting defects are reported to the MAH/sponsor and competent authorities.

Current Part I: `https://picscheme.org/docview/11332`

### C. PE 009-18 Annex 20 - Quality Risk Management

Annex 20 provides the QRM process framework across the product lifecycle: risk assessment, control, communication and review, together with tools and possible applications.

Important currentness note: the Annex 20 text in PE 009-18 is unchanged by the 2026 Annex 19 revision, is based on the earlier ICH Q9 framework, and states that it is not intended to create new regulatory expectations. Where a jurisdiction has adopted **ICH Q9(R1)**, use the jurisdiction's current adoption for R1 concepts such as formality, supply/availability risk, risk-based decision-making and management of subjectivity. Do not silently attribute R1-specific wording to PE 009-18 Annex 20.

Current Annexes: `https://picscheme.org/docview/11333`

### D. PIC/S inspection guidance - secondary, not a new legal duty

- **PI 038-2 Aide-Memoire on Assessment of Quality Risk Management Implementation:** inspectors consider whether QRM is integrated into the PQS, whether risk assessment/control/communication/review are implemented, whether QRM is misused to justify noncompliance, and whether periodic/triggered review keeps risk decisions current.
- **PI 054-1 Recommendation on How to Evaluate and Demonstrate the Effectiveness of a Pharmaceutical Quality System in Relation to Risk-based Change Management:** supports inspection of science- and risk-based change management and expressly states that it does not create a new GMP requirement.

Use these for inspection-readiness analysis, not as substitutes for PE 009 or national law.

## Practical MAH/holder lifecycle risk-governance model

The following is a professional synthesis, not a universal statutory template. Use it to test whether a company can demonstrate coherent lifecycle control across the requirements above.

### 1. Risk sources / inputs

At minimum consider, as applicable:
- development and registration commitments;
- process/product knowledge and CQAs/CPPs;
- deviations, OOS/OOT and investigations;
- change controls and regulatory variations;
- stability and CPV/process-capability signals;
- complaints, returns, quality defects and recalls;
- adverse reactions, safety signals, PSUR/PBRER/PASS and other PV outputs;
- supplier/CDMO/CRO/distributor/logistics performance;
- inspections, audits and self-inspections;
- pharmacopoeial/regulatory changes;
- supply continuity/shortage and product-availability risks;
- emerging scientific, epidemiologic or clinical information.

### 2. Risk record / register fields

For a MAH-level risk register or equivalent control, consider:
- product / market / lifecycle stage;
- risk statement and source;
- safety, efficacy, quality-controllability and supply dimensions as applicable;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- initial risk and rationale;
- existing controls;
- required actions, owner and due date;
- regulatory/PV/quality-reporting trigger;
- residual risk and acceptance authority;
- monitoring indicator / KRI;
- review trigger and review frequency;
- escalation / management-review status;
- closure evidence and linked CAPA/change/variation/RMP/PQR records.

Do not require a single enterprise risk-register document if the regulated system validly controls risks through linked records. Require traceability and governance, not a particular software form.

### 3. Governance cadence

- **China:** use NMPA No. 126/2022 Article 26 as a specific minimum governance anchor for recurring/emerging quality-risk review: in principle at least quarterly, with Quality Head leadership and company-head resource support.
- **EU:** use lifecycle RMP/PV updates, PQR, management review and event-driven quality/safety escalation as applicable; do not invent a universal quarterly MAH requirement.
- **US:** use regulatory/product commitments, postmarketing safety/FAR/annual-report clocks, APR/PQR, management review and event-driven escalation; do not invent a universal MAH quarterly review or universal RMP requirement.
- **PIC/S:** use PQS/QRM/PQR/self-inspection and event-driven review; do not convert inspector aide-memoire suggestions into a universal statutory calendar.

## Audit / inspection readiness questions

When auditing a holder risk-management system, ask:

1. Is the regulated legal role correctly identified in each market?
2. Is there a documented map of safety, efficacy, quality and supply risks, with clear ownership and interfaces?
3. Are product-safety RMS/RMP obligations separated from, but linked to, pharmaceutical QRM?
4. Can the holder show how new signals from complaints, PV, stability, deviations, CPV, suppliers and audits enter risk review?
5. Is uncertainty documented rather than hidden behind a numeric score?
6. Are changes assessed before implementation and routed to the correct regulatory category?
7. For outsourced work, can the holder demonstrate contractor capability assessment, information exchange, audit/oversight, performance review and escalation?
8. Are time-critical external reports assessed in parallel with root-cause investigation?
9. Do periodic reviews convert trends into CAPA, revalidation, regulatory variations, RMP/RMM updates, recall or other risk controls where needed?
10. Can senior management demonstrate awareness of significant/emerging risks and provision of adequate resources?
11. Is residual-risk acceptance authorised at an appropriate level and revisited when new information appears?
12. Can the organisation trace a major risk from initial signal through assessment, decision, action, effectiveness check and closure?

## Output rule for jurisdictional comparisons

When using this map, organise the answer by substantive risk-governance topic, not merely by clause number. For each topic state:

- jurisdiction and regulated role;
- legal or guidance source;
- exact requirement or regulatory expectation;
- whether it is safety/benefit-risk, quality/QRM, or both;
- lifecycle timing and trigger;
- holder/manufacturer/contract-party responsibility;
- evidence expected in the quality/PV system;
- compliance impact and any gap/action.

Always distinguish `Regulatory text`, `Regulatory interpretation`, and `Professional assessment`.
