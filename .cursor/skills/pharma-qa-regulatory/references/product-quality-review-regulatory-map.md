# Product Quality Review / Annual Quality Evaluation regulatory map

## Purpose and scope

Use this file for product-quality-review tasks covering finished medicinal products and APIs across China, the EU, PIC/S, the United States, the EAEU, and ICH. It distinguishes binding/regional GMP requirements from harmonised guidance and professional implementation practice.

**Currentness checkpoint:** 2026-09-30. Reopen the official source before relying on exact wording, current status, national implementation, or a jurisdiction-specific conclusion. During the 2026-09-30 check, the EAEU Legal Portal record for Decision No. 77 could not be fetched in the execution environment; the EAEU clause map below therefore relies on the Skill's previously verified Decision No. 77 routing baseline plus corroborating published text and must be revalidated against the official EAEU Legal Portal when current legal status is decisive.

## Terminology and interpretation controls

- Use `Product Quality Review (PQR)` for the China, EU, PIC/S and EAEU finished-product constructs when translating or comparing their GMP requirements.
- For US finished-drug CGMP, 21 CFR 211.180(e) requires an evaluation, **at least annually**, of the quality standards of each drug product. `Annual Product Review (APR)` is common industry shorthand, but it is not the title of the CFR requirement. Do not state that FDA's CFR text is identical to EU/PIC/S PQR.
- For APIs, ICH Q7 Section 2.5 uses `Product Quality Review`; regional Part II GMP texts commonly implement the same Q7 structure. Verify regional adoption before treating ICH text itself as binding law.
- `APQR` is a company/industry abbreviation used for annual product quality review. Do not treat it as a universal statutory term.
- Separate the review **frequency**, **minimum regulatory inputs**, **responsibility**, **evaluation/conclusion**, and **follow-up actions**. A company may use one global PQR template that exceeds local minimums, but do not mislabel the global template as the minimum legal content in every market.

## API PQR minimum content under ICH Q7

ICH Q7 2.50-2.51 provides a narrower API-specific minimum than the finished-product EU/PIC/S PQR list. Include at least:

1. critical in-process control and critical API test results;
2. batches failing established specifications;
3. critical deviations or non-conformances and related investigations;
4. process or analytical-method changes;
5. stability-monitoring results;
6. quality-related returns, complaints and recalls; and
7. adequacy of corrective actions.

Then evaluate whether corrective action or revalidation is needed, document the reasons, and complete agreed actions in a timely and effective manner. Do not automatically import finished-product topics such as marketing-authorisation variations or post-marketing commitments into an API PQR as a claimed Q7 minimum; include them when regional rules, quality agreements, customer/regulatory commitments or the company's integrated system require them.

## Responsibility and outsourced-manufacturing rules

### China

- GMP Art. 23 assigns the head of quality management responsibility to ensure that PQRs are performed.
- SAMR Order No. 28 Art. 44 applies the annual product-quality-review duty to both the MAH and drug manufacturer.
- GMP Art. 268 requires a written technical agreement between contract giver and contract acceptor defining PQR responsibilities and ensuring timely completion.
- For a China MAH with contract manufacture, design the procedure so MAH oversight does not collapse into passive receipt of the CMO's report. Distinguish data generation, report preparation, quality review, MAH evaluation, approval/acceptance, action ownership and closure. The exact allocation is company governance unless a specific regulation or agreement assigns it.

### EU / PIC/S / EAEU

- Part I 1.11 requires the manufacturer and, where different, the MAH/registration holder to evaluate the review results.
- Where the MAH/holder is not the manufacturer, define PQR responsibilities in a technical agreement.
- Scientifically justified grouping by product type is permitted in the current EU/PIC/S/EAEU-aligned text. Do not interpret grouping as permission to lose product-specific visibility for critical signals, complaints, stability, deviations, changes or commitments.

### United States

- 21 CFR 211.180(e) requires written procedures for the annual evaluation and specifies minimum data categories, but it does not create the same MAH-versus-manufacturer PQR allocation language found in EU/PIC/S 1.11.
- For contract manufacture, assess Part 210/211 responsibilities and applicable quality agreements separately. Do not import EU MAH/PQR wording into US CGMP as though it were a CFR requirement.

## Frequency, rolling review, grouping and low-volume products

- **China:** Art. 266 and SAMR Order No. 28 Art. 44 establish an annual review expectation. China GMP permits dosage-form grouping where scientifically justified, but the review must still support meaningful product/process conclusions.
- **EU / PIC/S:** current effective Chapter 1 permits regular periodic or rolling reviews, normally documented annually, and scientifically justified grouping by product type.
- **US:** 21 CFR 211.180(e) requires evaluation at least annually for each drug product; the core clause explicitly allows a representative number of batches rather than requiring every batch to be re-reviewed as a discrete PQR record.
- **ICH Q7:** API quality reviews should normally be conducted and documented annually; Q7 Q&A 2.5 also states that annual frequency is generally expected.
- **Low-volume / no-batch periods:** the current effective EU/PIC/S Chapter 1 text does not contain the detailed clarifications proposed in the 2025 revised draft. The draft specifically seeks to clarify product grouping and situations with only a limited number of batches. Treat those draft clarifications as a future-change signal only; do not enforce them as current GMP until adopted and effective.

## PQR-to-PQS decision logic

When reviewing or designing a PQR, preserve the following distinction:

1. **Data compilation** - establish a complete, traceable population and review period.
2. **Trend and signal evaluation** - determine whether data show adverse, recurrent or emerging patterns; use fit-for-purpose statistical methods where useful, but do not invent regulatory statistical thresholds.
3. **State-of-control assessment** - assess process consistency, specification suitability, stability, validation/qualification status and recurring quality risks.
4. **Regulatory / registered-state reconciliation** - assess approved or reported changes and commitments where the governing framework requires it.
5. **Action decision** - determine the need for CAPA, change control, requalification/revalidation, additional monitoring, supplier/contractor action or regulatory assessment.
6. **Management follow-up** - assign owners/due dates, track completion and effectiveness, and escalate persistent or systemic signals into management review and the broader PQS.

Label steps 2-6 as `Professional assessment / implementation design` where the exact mechanism is not prescribed by the governing clause.

## Inspection-readiness review checklist

For a PQR/APR inspection-readiness or SOP review, check at least:

- review period, product/strength/site scope and rationale for any grouping;
- complete batch census and reconciliation of released, rejected, reprocessed/reworked and other relevant batches;
- data-source traceability and treatment of missing, late or corrected data;
- trend method, denominators, stratification and rationale for exclusions;
- OOS/OOT/deviation/investigation recurrence and root-cause patterns;
- CAPA effectiveness and overdue/repeated actions;
- process, analytical, material/supplier and registered-state changes;
- stability trends and ongoing commitments;
- complaints, returns, recalls and market-quality signals;
- equipment/utilities qualification and validation/continued-verification status where applicable;
- quality/technical agreement currency and outsourced-data completeness;
- prior PQR commitments and carry-over actions;
- conclusion on state of control, specification suitability and need for CAPA/change/revalidation; and
- documented review/approval and evidence that required actions entered the controlled quality system.

This checklist is a professional synthesis for implementation and inspection readiness. Do not present every line as a statutory minimum in every jurisdiction.

## Current-change alerts

- **EU Chapter 1:** the European Commission's revised Chapter 1 consultation closed in 2025. The draft aligns with ICH Q9(R1) and includes clarifications for PQR grouping and limited-batch situations. As of the 2026-09-30 checkpoint, the operative human-medicinal-product Chapter 1 remains the version in operation since 31 January 2013.
- **PIC/S Chapter 1:** PIC/S still lists `Draft guidelines: Revised Chapter 1 - Pharmaceutical Quality System` under Drafts. PE 009-18 is current; its 2026 revision was driven by Annex 19, not adoption of the draft Chapter 1.
- **EAEU:** recheck Decision No. 77 and amendments against the official EAEU Legal Portal before an EAEU inspection or filing decision; do not rely on similarity to EU/PIC/S as a substitute for Union-level legal verification.
