## 1. Purpose, scope, and currentness

Use this reference for human medicinal-product recall governance, recall SOP/SMP review, recall classification, health-hazard/risk assessment, recall initiation, authority communication, recall depth, consignee communication, effectiveness checks, reconciliation, disposition, recall termination, CAPA, PQR/APR feedback, and MAH/application-holder/recalling-firm/manufacturer responsibility mapping in China, the United States, and the European Union.

Recall-specific sources in this file were currentness-checked against official or government sources on **2026-09-24**. This file is a core commercial-human-drug baseline. ## 2. Core legal distinctions and terminology controls

Do not translate or map recall classifications one-for-one across jurisdictions.

| Concept | China | United States | European Union |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary recall construct | MAH recall of marketed drug with quality problem or other safety hazard | Firm-initiated or FDA-requested recall under 21 CFR Part 7; special statutory mandatory-recall authorities exist only for specified product categories | MAH/manufacturer recall or authority-ordered market withdrawal within EU legal/GMP and national competent-authority systems |
| Health-risk classification | Level I / II / III recall under NMPA Recall Measures | FDA Class I / II / III classification under 21 CFR 7.3 and 7.41 | Quality-defect/recall classification under EU/EEA authority procedures; do not invent a universal EU `Class I/II/III` equivalent |
| Related but distinct action | Stop sale/use, production suspension, corrective handling, ordered recall | Correction, market withdrawal, stock recovery, public warning, FDA-requested recall | Withdrawal from market, prohibition of supply, rapid alert, public communication, other risk-mitigating market action |
| Who assigns formal recall class | MAH determines recall level from investigation/risk assessment subject to regulatory oversight | FDA assigns Class I/II/III | Competent-authority/Union procedure and national implementation; company may propose risk assessment/action |

### 2.1 United States definitions must remain distinct

Under 21 CFR 7.3:

- `Recall` is a firm's removal or correction of a marketed product that FDA considers violative and subject to legal action; it excludes market withdrawal and stock recovery.
- `Correction` is repair, modification, adjustment, relabeling, destruction, inspection, or related action without physical removal to another location.
- `Market withdrawal` involves a minor violation not subject to FDA legal action or no violation.
- `Stock recovery` concerns product that has not been marketed or left the firm's direct control.
- `Recall strategy` is the planned course of action covering recall depth, public warning, and effectiveness checks.

Do not call every US product removal a recall.

### 2.2 European Union terms require authority-route context

Use `recall`, `withdrawal from the market`, `prohibition of supply`, `quality defect`, and `rapid alert` according to the specific legal or Union-procedure context. Do not assume that a national competent authority uses identical administrative terminology or identical forms to EMA for a centrally authorised product.

### 2.3 China terminology

Use the statutory `一级召回 / 二级召回 / 三级召回` classification only for recall classification under the current Drug Recall Administrative Measures. Do not use a company's internal `Critical/Major/Minor` quality-event grade as a substitute.

## 3. Six-layer recall control model

For any recall task, separate the following six layers:

1. **Trigger and immediate containment** — identify the defect/safety hazard, distribution status, affected lots/markets, stop-sale/use/ship needs, and whether urgent authority contact is required.
2. **Health-hazard / risk assessment** — evaluate severity, probability, exposed population, vulnerable groups, reversibility, detectability where relevant, and supply/shortage consequences.
3. **Recall classification and strategy** — determine or propose recall class, scope, depth, consignee level, public warning, and effectiveness approach under the jurisdiction's own framework.
4. **Regulatory communication and execution** — notify the regulator and supply chain using the required route and timeline; maintain distribution traceability and execute retrieval/correction.
5. **Effectiveness, reconciliation, and disposition** — verify receipt/action by consignees, reconcile distributed/returned/corrected quantities, control recalled stock, and disposition/rework/destroy only under compliant controls.
6. **Termination and quality-system closure** — obtain regulatory termination/closeout where applicable; complete investigation, CAPA, change/regulatory assessment, PQR/APR/management-review feedback, and recurrence monitoring.

Do not wait for final root cause when a separate recall, public-health, quality-defect, FAR, BPDR, or PV trigger already requires action.

## 4. China source map and mandatory timelines

### 4.1 Drug Administration Law of the People's Republic of China

Use the current Drug Administration Law as the statutory top layer.

Key recall-linked provisions:

- Article 12: national drug-traceability and pharmacovigilance systems support recall traceability and safety control.
- Article 77: MAH post-marketing risk-management plan and continued lifecycle management.
- Articles 80-81: post-marketing adverse-reaction monitoring and risk-control interface.
- **Article 82**: when a drug has a quality problem or other safety hazard, the MAH shall immediately stop selling, inform relevant distributors and medical institutions to stop sale/use, recall sold product, promptly disclose recall information, stop manufacture when necessary, and report recall/disposition to the provincial drug regulator and health authority; regulators can order recall if the MAH fails to recall when required.
- Article 83: post-marketing evaluation may lead to market risk-control decisions.
- **Article 135**: refusal to recall after an authority order can result in penalties, including a fine calculated on recall-product value and, in serious cases, revocation of approvals/licenses; manufacturers, distributors, and medical institutions refusing to cooperate may also be penalised.

Official checkpoint: `https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_b73ec9dabb2d468281d6c994b4bcdaf8.html`

### 4.2 Regulations for Implementation of the Drug Administration Law, State Council Decree No. 828 (2026 revision)

The 2026 revision became effective on 2026-05-15. For recall work, use it as the administrative-regulation layer around lifecycle risk control, post-marketing evaluation, MAH responsibilities, contract manufacture, and regulatory response.

Recall-linked provisions include:

- **Article 28**: the MAH shall periodically perform post-marketing evaluation and may take measures including label revision, higher quality standards, process improvement, suspension of manufacture/sale, **drug recall**, or application to cancel the registration certificate according to the evaluation outcome.
- **Article 61**: a medical institution that identifies a quality problem in a purchased/used drug shall immediately control risk, promptly inform the supplier and MAH, and report to the local drug regulator.
- **Article 64**: MAHs, manufacturers, distributors, and medical institutions shall provide traceability information as required, supporting recall scope determination and reconciliation.
- **Article 70**: where regulators impose suspension of manufacture, sale, use, or import as a risk-control measure, the regulated entities shall rectify and eliminate the safety hazard before the authority lifts the control.
- **Article 73**: an entity where a drug-safety event occurs shall immediately take measures to prevent expansion of harm and immediately report the event to the local drug regulator.

These provisions support recall detection, traceability, emergency containment, and lifecycle risk control; they do not replace the specific recall procedure and clocks in the 2022 Drug Recall Administrative Measures.

Official checkpoint: `https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202601/content_7056255.htm`

Do not import historical pre-2026 implementation-regulation wording when the current 2026 provision applies.

### 4.3 Drug Recall Administrative Measures, NMPA Announcement No. 92 of 2022

This is the core China recall procedure. At the 2026-09-24 checkpoint it remained current, with an implementation date of 2022-11-01.

#### Scope and responsibility

- Arts. 2-5: define recall, quality problem/other safety hazard, and MAH responsibility; manufacturers, distributors, and use institutions must cooperate.
- Art. 6: when manufacturers, distributors, or use institutions identify a potential quality problem or safety hazard, they must timely inform the MAH, take necessary risk controls, and report as applicable.
- Art. 7: traceability and complete purchase/sales records must support recall.
- Art. 9: recall-information disclosure obligations apply to regulators and MAHs.

#### Investigation, risk assessment, and classification

- Arts. 10-12: collect and investigate quality, adverse-event, storage/transport, process/change, use, population, lot, quantity, distribution, and other relevant risk data.
- Art. 13: classify recall by health impact:
  - Level I: use may have caused or may cause serious health harm;
  - Level II: use may have caused or may cause temporary or reversible health harm;
  - Level III: use generally will not cause health harm but product needs to be recovered for other reasons.
- Art. 14: prepare an investigation/risk-assessment report and recall plan.

#### Active-recall clocks

Once the MAH decides to recall:

| Requirement | Level I | Level II | Level III |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Send recall notification and file investigation/risk report, recall plan, and notice with provincial regulator | within **1 day** | within **3 days** | within **7 days** |
| Progress report to provincial regulator during recall | **daily** | every **3 days** | every **7 days** |

Additional controls:

- Art. 15: implement recall immediately after determination; publish recall information; Level I/II recalls also require application for publication on the provincial regulator website.
- Arts. 18-19: segregate and control recalled stock; preserve required storage conditions; destruction must occur under the specified supervision route; certain corrective handling may be permitted when compliant and scientifically justified.
- Art. 19: recall-disposition records must be retained **5 years and no less than 1 year after product expiry**.
- Art. 20: after recall completion, report recall and disposition to the provincial drug regulator and health authority within **10 working days**; include recall in the drug annual report.
- Art. 21: for specified foreign recalls affecting a product related to China, the domestic responsible entity must report specified foreign-recall information within **10 working days** after overseas recall initiation; assess whether China recall is also required.
- Arts. 22-28: authority-ordered recall route, MAH execution, progress reporting, summary report, authority effectiveness evaluation, and re-recall when risk has not been adequately controlled.
- Art. 28: provincial regulator reviews the summary and recall effectiveness within **10 working days** after receipt and can require a renewed recall.
- Art. 30: vaccines marketed in China use this recall procedure, while suspected vaccine quality-issue handling also follows the Vaccine Administration Law.
- Art. 31: export-drug quality/safety recalls must follow importing-market law and contract requirements, with timely communication to the importing authority and purchaser.

Official NMPA source address: `https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/xzhgfxwj/20221026164304199.html`
Government mirror with current-status metadata: `https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=96076`

### 4.4 China GMP and related quality-system sources

Use *Good Manufacturing Practice for Drugs (2010 Revision)* together with the recall measures.

Recall-linked routing includes:

- Art. 266 PQR review of quality-related returns, complaints, recalls, and investigations;
- Arts. 271-277 complaint/defect investigation and potential-recall interface;
- Art. 290 contract-manufacturing access to complaint, suspected-defect, and recall information;
- Arts. 293-305 recall system, responsibilities, distribution records, traceability, written procedures, periodic effectiveness evaluation, returned/recalled product control, reconciliation, final reporting, and retained evidence.

Also use NMPA Announcement No. 126 of 2022 for MAH recall governance, especially the requirement to establish a recall system and act when quality problems or other safety hazards are identified.

When distribution entities are involved, use the current China GSP for recall assistance, stop-sale/recovery controls, and records.

### 4.5 China decision rule

Do not use a fixed `24/48/72 hour` recall clock imported from the old recall regime or another market. The current 2022 measures use the **1-day / 3-day / 7-day** notification framework after the MAH recall decision, plus the progress and completion clocks above.

## 5. United States source map and legal-status distinctions

### 5.1 Core policy: 21 CFR Part 7, Subpart C

For human drugs, treat 21 CFR Part 7 Subpart C as the core FDA recall policy framework. Most drug recalls operate as firm-initiated voluntary recalls or FDA-requested recalls. Do **not** imply that FDA has one universal statutory mandatory-recall authority for all human drugs.

At the 2026-09-24 checkpoint, the eCFR Part 7 provisions remained current. Use the exact current eCFR text at execution time.

Key sections:

- **21 CFR 7.3** — definitions, including recall, correction, market withdrawal, stock recovery, recall strategy, and FDA recall classification.
- **7.40** — recall policy and purpose.
- **7.41** — FDA health-hazard evaluation and recall classification factors; FDA assigns Class I, II, or III.
- **7.42** — recall strategy; factors include health-hazard evaluation, product identification, obviousness of defect, amount remaining in market, and continued availability of essential product; recall depth can be consumer/user, retail, or wholesale; strategy also covers public warning and effectiveness checks.
- **7.45** — FDA-requested recall when a distributed product presents risk of illness/injury or gross consumer deception, the firm has not initiated recall, and FDA action is necessary to protect public health/welfare.
- **7.46** — firm-initiated recall; a firm that removes/corrects because it believes product is violative is requested to notify FDA immediately and provide product, reason, discovery, risk, production/distribution, communication, strategy, and contact information. The firm need not delay initiation while FDA reviews classification/strategy.
- **7.49** — recall communication to affected direct accounts, including identification, stop-distribution/use instructions, downstream notification when appropriate, disposition instructions, and response mechanism.
- **7.50** — FDA public notification through the Enforcement Report, subject to limited circumstances for delayed public notification.
- **7.53** — recall status reports; FDA determines frequency based on urgency and generally uses an interval of **2-4 weeks** unless otherwise specified; reports include consignees notified/responding/nonresponding, returned/corrected quantities, effectiveness checks, and estimated completion.
- **7.55** — FDA termination when reasonable efforts have been made and removal/correction/disposition is commensurate with the hazard; the recalling firm may request termination with current status and disposition information.
- **7.59** — general preparedness guidance: maintain a written contingency plan, adequate lot coding, and distribution records to facilitate recall.

Official Part 7 checkpoint: `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-7/subpart-C`

### 5.2 Finished-drug CGMP interfaces

For finished drugs under Part 211, recall capability depends on several enforceable CGMP controls:

- **21 CFR 211.150(b)**: establish a system by which distribution of each drug-product lot can be readily determined to facilitate recall if necessary.
- **211.198**: written complaint procedures, QCU review, investigation decision, and complaint records.
- **211.192**: thorough investigation and extension to associated batches/products where appropriate.
- **211.180(e)-(f)**: annual review and management notification interfaces involving recalls/investigations.
- **211.204 and 211.208**, where applicable: returned-drug and salvaging controls can affect disposition of recovered product; inspect exact applicability before citing them in a recall SOP.

### 5.3 Application-holder and biologic reporting interfaces

Recall is separate from other time-critical FDA reporting. Evaluate in parallel:

- **21 CFR 314.81(b)(1)** and **314.98**: Field Alert Reports for qualifying information on distributed NDA/ANDA products; this is a separate early-warning obligation and not a recall-classification rule.
- **21 CFR 600.14**: Biological Product Deviation reporting for qualifying licensed biologics events.
- **21 CFR 314.80 / 600.80**: postmarketing safety-reporting interface when the event contains adverse-experience information.

Do not wait for recall classification or final root cause when a separate reporting clock is already triggered.

### 5.4 FDA current guidance documents

Use these as nonbinding FDA recommendations, not as CFR requirements:

- *Initiation of Voluntary Recalls Under 21 CFR Part 7, Subpart C*, Final Guidance, March 2022 — recall preparedness and timely initiation.
- *Product Recalls, Including Removals and Corrections*, Final Guidance, March 2020 — information provided to FDA, recall documentation, customer notification, and recall handling.
- *Public Warning and Notification of Recalls Under 21 CFR Part 7, Subpart C*, Final Guidance, February 2019 — public-warning decision, content, and coordination.

Current FDA recall resources page: `https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/industry-guidance-recalls`

### 5.5 FDA internal enforcement sources

Use FDA's **2026 Investigations Operations Manual Chapter 7 - Recall Activities** and the current **Regulatory Procedures Manual Chapter 7** as regulator-operational/inspection context only. They do not create new firm obligations beyond the governing statutes/regulations, but they are useful for inspection readiness, recall information packages, classification workflow, distribution-list expectations, audit checks, and effectiveness verification.

Do not state FDA internal target times for agency personnel as company legal deadlines.

### 5.6 Special statutory mandatory-recall authority

For controlled substances, assess **FD&C Act section 569D / 21 U.S.C. 360bbb-8d** on notification, nondistribution, and recall of controlled substances. This is product-specific and does not create a universal mandatory-recall power for all prescription drugs.

Official checkpoint: `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title21-section360bbb-8d`

### 5.7 APIs and intermediates

For APIs/intermediates, use FDA/ICH Q7 Section 15 as guidance for complaints and recalls. Do not apply 21 CFR 211.198 mechanically to API operations outside its scope.

## 6. European Union source map and authority-routing distinctions

### 6.1 Directive 2001/83/EC

Use the current consolidated text as the principal legal layer for human medicinal products.

Key recall-linked provisions:

- **Article 117**: Member States must take appropriate steps to prohibit supply and withdraw medicinal products from the market when specified grounds apply, including harmfulness, lack of efficacy, unfavourable benefit-risk balance, incorrect composition, or specified control/manufacturing-authorisation failures. Action may be limited to affected batches.
- **Article 117a**: Member States must maintain a system for suspected falsified products, suspected quality defects, MAH recalls, and authority-ordered withdrawals across the supply chain, including outside normal working hours; where serious public-health risk is suspected, a rapid alert is transmitted without delay and, where products are deemed to have reached patients, urgent public announcements are issued within **24 hours** to recall them from patients.
- **Article 123(2)-(2c)**: MAH must notify concerned Member States forthwith of actions to suspend marketing, withdraw a product, request withdrawal of marketing authorisation, or not renew, with reasons; corresponding notification applies to specified third-country actions and to the Agency where the action is based on Article 116 or 117(1) grounds.

Official consolidated checkpoint: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02001L0083-20250101`

### 6.2 Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 for centrally authorised products

For centrally authorised medicinal products, also consider the current consolidated Regulation (EC) No 726/2004:

- **Article 14b**: the MAH shall notify EMA **forthwith** of action it takes to suspend marketing, withdraw a medicinal product from the market, request withdrawal of the marketing authorisation, or not apply for renewal, together with the reasons; specified third-country actions based on Directive 2001/83/EC Articles 116 or 117(1) are also notified.
- **Article 20**: supervisory/competent authorities use the Union escalation route where manufacturing/import obligations are not met or measures under Titles IX/XI of Directive 2001/83/EC are considered. Treat this primarily as authority/supervisory routing, not as an independent company recall clock.

Use Article 14b together with Directive 2001/83/EC Article 123 and the current EMA quality-defect reporting route; do not duplicate or substitute notifications without checking the CAP procedure.

Official checkpoint: current consolidated EUR-Lex record for Regulation (EC) No 726/2004.

### 6.3 Commission Directive (EU) 2017/1572, Article 13

Article 13 provides the GMP legal basis for complaint recording/review and an effective system for recalling medicinal products **promptly and at any time** from the distribution network. A defect that could result in a recall or abnormal restriction on supply must be notified to the competent authority and, where applicable, the MAH; destination countries should be identified where possible. Recalls are linked to Article 123 of Directive 2001/83/EC.

Official checkpoint: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2017/1572/oj/eng`

### 6.4 EU GMP Part I Chapter 8

Use the currently applicable Chapter 8, *Complaints, Quality Defects and Product Recalls*, together with Chapter 1 QRM/PQS principles.

The Chapter 8 control model includes:

- documented procedures and trained responsible personnel;
- investigation and quality-risk assessment of quality defects/complaints;
- evaluation of other batches/products and defect extent;
- root cause and CAPA;
- timely competent-authority notification of quality defects that could lead to recall or abnormal restriction of supply;
- recall decision and execution with appropriate authority consultation;
- distribution information and ability to initiate recall promptly and at any time;
- effectiveness review, reconciliation, and documentation;
- periodic evaluation of recall arrangements and use of mock recall where appropriate under the quality system.

Official current index: `https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/eudralex/eudralex-volume-4_en`

Do not use a draft Chapter 8 revision as current GMP unless it has formally entered into operation.

### 6.5 EU GDP 2013/C 343/01, Chapter 6

For wholesale/distribution actors, use Chapter 6, *Complaints, Returns, Suspected Falsified Medicinal Products and Medicinal Product Recalls*.

Core controls include written procedures and records; distinction between quality and distribution complaints; prompt escalation of potential quality defects to manufacturer/MAH; recall arrangements and cooperation across the supply chain; and ready availability of records to competent authorities.

Official checkpoint: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=oj:JOC_2013_343_R_0001_01`

### 6.6 EMA/HMA Compilation of Union Procedures - 2026 versions

For cross-border/authority quality-defect and rapid-alert execution, use the current 2026 Union procedures rather than a generic reference to an unspecified `current compilation`.

At the 2026-09-24 checkpoint:

1. **Management and classification of reports of suspected quality defects in medicinal products and risk-based decision-making**
   - Date of adoption: **20 April 2026**
   - Entry into force: **May 2026**
   - Document version: **2**
   - Supersedes: version adopted May 2023
   - Revision includes timelines for different quality-defect types, ICH Q9(R1)-related changes, and addition of former Rapid Alert Appendix 1.
   - Official PDF: `https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/management-classification-reports-suspected-quality-defects-medicinal-products-risk-based-decision-making_en.pdf`

2. **Management of rapid alerts arising from quality defects risk assessment**
   - Date of adoption: **20 April 2026**
   - Entry into force: **May 2026**
   - Document version: **2**
   - Supersedes: version adopted 15 June 2024
   - Appendix 1 moved to the Quality Defect procedure.
   - Official PDF: `https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/management-rapid-alerts-arising-quality-defects-risk-assessment_en.pdf`

These are authority-network operational procedures. Use them to understand classification, coordinated risk decisions, rapid alerts, and cross-border handling. Do not present regulator-internal routing steps as standalone MAH legal obligations unless a separate legal/GMP source creates that obligation.

The 2026 quality-defect procedure includes NCA management targets such as risk-based classification within one working day when information is complete and risk-level management limits of High Risk 3 working days (potentially 1 working day for major public-health impact), Moderate Risk 5 working days, and Low Risk 10 working days (optional). **These are competent-authority quality-defect management timelines, not universal MAH recall-notification deadlines.** A company must still comply with the separate legal/GMP requirement for prompt reporting/action and any applicable NCA/EMA instructions.

The direct 2026 Rapid Alert PDF is Version 2, adopted 20 April 2026 and effective May 2026. If the EMA index page displays stale metadata for that document, rely on the current PDF metadata after verifying the file itself and record the discrepancy.

### 6.7 Centrally authorised products: EMA quality-defect reporting

For centrally authorised products, use EMA's current `Quality defects and recalls` and `Reporting a quality defect to EMA` routes.

Operational points include:

- report quality defects through the current EMA route;
- if final investigation/health-hazard reports are unavailable at initial notification, provide interim reports under the EMA instructions; at the 2026-09-24 checkpoint EMA requested interim reports no later than **48 hours after original notification** until final reports are available;
- if recall is proposed, provide defect classification and proposed recall action/scope information;
- keep EMA informed of new information immediately.

These EMA CAP instructions are not a universal `48-hour recall deadline` for all EU products. Nationally authorised products follow the relevant national competent-authority route.

Official CAP reporting checkpoint: `https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/compliance-post-authorisation/quality-defects-recalls/reporting-quality-defect-ema`

## 7. Cross-jurisdiction comparison

| Topic | China | United States | European Union |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary legal actor | MAH | Recalling firm; applicant/manufacturer roles also matter for CGMP/reporting | MAH + manufacturer + competent authority, depending on duty |
| Formal classification | MAH Level I/II/III under Recall Measures | FDA Class I/II/III | Union/NCA risk classification; no direct statutory China/US class mapping |
| Recall initiation | Immediate once MAH determines quality problem/safety hazard requiring recall | Firm may initiate; do not wait for FDA strategy/classification review | Prompt action with competent-authority communication; route depends on product authorisation and Member State/CAP |
| Fixed notification clock | Yes: 1/3/7 days after recall decision for recall notice/filing | No universal `1/3/7 day` or `24-hour` recall clock in Part 7; firm believed-violative removal/correction should be notified to FDA immediately under 7.46 | No single universal company `X-hour recall clock`; Article 117a has a 24-hour public-announcement rule when serious-risk product reached patients; CAP quality-defect process has separate reporting expectations |
| Progress reports | Daily / every 3 days / every 7 days by recall level | FDA specifies; generally 2-4 week interval under 7.53 unless otherwise specified | Authority/route-specific; follow NCA/EMA requirements |
| Termination | MAH completion report + regulator effectiveness review; re-recall may be required | FDA terminates recall or firm requests termination | Authority/MAH closeout route varies; document effectiveness and final action |
| Traceability | Statutory drug traceability + recall measures | 211.150(b) lot distribution system + Part 7 preparedness | GMP/GDP distribution records and recall capability |

## 8. Product- and lifecycle-specific extensions

Before finalizing a recall answer, check whether one of these applies:

### China

- Vaccines: Recall Measures apply, but suspected vaccine-quality-issue disposition also follows the Vaccine Administration Law.
- Blood products, narcotics/psychotropics, radiopharmaceuticals, and other specially controlled products: load their additional regulatory controls.
- Export products: apply Recall Measures Art. 31 and importing-market requirements.

### United States

- Controlled substances: assess FD&C Act section 569D / 21 U.S.C. 360bbb-8d.
- Biologics: assess 21 CFR 600.14, 600.80, and applicable biologics requirements.
- Combination products/devices: device mandatory-recall/correction-reporting requirements can apply; do not use this human-drug baseline alone.
- APIs: use ICH/FDA Q7 complaint/recall guidance where applicable.

### European Union

- Centrally authorised versus nationally authorised products: reporting route differs.
- Investigational medicinal products: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1569 Article 14 requires manufacturer/sponsor complaint and recall arrangements and authority notification for defects that could result in recall or abnormal restriction of supply.
- National competent-authority procedures can add forms, contacts, and immediate operational expectations.

## 9. Inspection and SOP review checkpoints

A recall SOP/SMP should permit an auditor to determine, without inference:

1. what constitutes a recall versus other market action;
2. who has legal decision authority and who can act outside business hours;
3. how quality defect, PV, FAR/BPDR, falsification, shortage, and recall decisions run in parallel;
4. how initial containment is documented before full investigation is complete;
5. how affected lots, markets, countries, and related products are scoped;
6. how health-hazard/risk assessment is documented and updated;
7. how jurisdiction-specific classification is assigned or proposed;
8. how recall depth and public communication are determined;
9. how distribution data are generated rapidly and reconciled;
10. how distributors, logistics providers, contract manufacturers, and medical institutions are notified;
11. how statutory/regulatory clocks are tracked independently of internal investigation deadlines;
12. how recalled product is segregated, stored, transported, corrected, reworked, destroyed, or otherwise disposed;
13. how effectiveness checks are designed and escalated if response is incomplete;
14. how total distributed, on-hand, returned, corrected, destroyed, and unaccounted quantities are reconciled;
15. how regulator status reports and final reports are prepared and approved;
16. how recall termination/closure is distinguished from investigation/CAPA closure;
17. how recall lessons feed PQR/APR, management review, supplier/contractor oversight, validation, change control, training, and risk management;
18. how mock recall/periodic recall-system testing is planned and documented under applicable GMP/PQS expectations.

Do not close a recall solely because all returned units have been received. Close only when the jurisdiction-specific regulatory/quality-system termination criteria, product disposition, reconciliation, investigation/CAPA obligations, and residual-risk controls are satisfied.
