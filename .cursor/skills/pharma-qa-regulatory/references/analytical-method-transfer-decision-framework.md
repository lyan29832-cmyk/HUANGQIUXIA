# Analytical method transfer: decisions and acceptance

## Contents
- Scope and source boundary
- Strategy selection
- Protocol and criteria
- Investigation and closure

## Scope and source boundary
Use for QC, stability, in-process and cleaning-residue analytical methods. Separate transfer, validation, verification, method modification and biologics comparability. Use technology-transfer-regulatory-map.md for official sources. WHO 12.24 describes confirmation testing, interlaboratory comparability, co-validation and paper-based knowledge transfer as possible scientifically justified risk-based approaches. The decision rules below are professional controls; they do not establish a universal legal transfer waiver.

## Strategy selection
First establish intended use, lifecycle stage, applicable dossier version, method validation status, measurand and range, matrix, specification/decision limits, and both laboratories' capability. Then decide:

| Strategy | Candidate situation | Necessary evidence and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interlaboratory comparative transfer | Mature method; both units can execute; site differences need experimental evaluation | Representative common samples, comparable standards, defined analysts/days/runs and independent preparations, preapproved comparison criteria; do not use replicate injections as independent preparations. |
| Receiving-lab confirmation/verification | Existing validated/compendial procedure with justified limited verification need | Demonstrate intended-use suitability in actual matrix/range and instrument configuration; a compendial title or previous use alone does not establish suitability. |
| Co-validation | Development/validation and transfer can be prospectively integrated across both labs | Both units contribute defined evidence to one approved plan; confirm receiving-site capability, data ownership and complete validation coverage. |
| Supplementary/partial or full revalidation plus transfer | Inadequate validation or changes to principle, matrix, range, sample preparation or critical instrument conditions | Define affected performance characteristics; conduct necessary validation work; separately establish RU implementation capability. Revalidation is not automatically a substitute for transfer. |
| Paper-based knowledge transfer / documented reduction of experimental comparison | Existing comparable capability and strong prior evidence support limited additional work | Written rationale, exact method/version/intended use, staff/instrument equivalence, prior raw data and training, remaining verification needs, QA decision and regulatory compatibility. It cannot excuse absent evidence, required protocol or local verification. |

Avoid a paper-only route for novel/unstable methods, unresolved atypical data, low-level impurities near decision limits, complex bioassays or material equipment/matrix differences without strong supporting evidence. This is a professional risk rule, not a categorical legal prohibition. If the SU no longer exists, retrieve source validation/raw data and consider reference-laboratory bridging and RU validation/verification; explain inability to conduct direct comparisons instead of fabricating them.

## Protocol and criteria
Create an approved protocol before execution. Include objective; scope; method/specification/version and dossier reconciliation; SU/RU responsibilities; prerequisite validation gaps; instruments/software/configuration; training and competency; reference-standard identity, potency, qualification and expiry; sample source, homogeneity, stability, shipping and storage; study design and justification; raw-data access; statistical evaluation; acceptance criteria; deviation/OOS/OOT handling; amendments; reports and approvals.

Use a criteria record with fields: characteristic; intended decision; prior validation evidence; expected concentration/matrix; allowable difference or equivalence margin; rationale; design/sample number; analysis; failure action. Do not invent universal percentages, t-test cutoffs, numbers of analysts, batches or days.

| Method class | Professional design considerations |
| --- | --- |
| Assay/content | Laboratory bias, preparation variability, concentration range, reference potency and recovery; evaluate precision and bias appropriate to intended use. |
| Impurities/degradation | Specificity, relevant impurities, quantitation capability near reporting/specification limits, response factors, integration, carryover and sample stability. Passing main-peak assay does not establish impurity-method suitability. |
| Dissolution | Apparatus configuration, hydrodynamics, deaeration, sink/sample filtration, timed sampling and dosage-unit variability. Do not impose f2 indiscriminately as an analytical-transfer criterion. |
| Identification | Discriminatory ability, relevant positive/negative or interference samples; do not force quantitative statistics onto qualitative decisions. |
| Bioassay/potency | Reference alignment, cell/reagent history, curve/model fit, parallelism where relevant, run validity and analyst/intermediate variability. |
| Microbiological methods | Organism/matrix suitability, recovery, inhibition/neutralization, incubation and controls; method-specific regulated expectations control. |
| Cleaning residues | Recovery from receiving-equipment materials, swab/rinse technique, specificity, sensitivity against justified residue limits and cleaning-agent interference. |

Statistical significance alone is not practical equivalence. Choose a justified equivalence/bias/precision assessment appropriate to data assumptions and decision risk; report confidence and uncertainty. System suitability is necessary where prescribed but is not the transfer success criterion by itself.

## Investigation and closure
If criteria fail or results are atypical, preserve all data; assess sample integrity, standards, preparation, instrument/software, integration, method robustness, analysts and site effects; investigate with defined scope and approval. Do not retest until success or revise criteria after seeing results without documented scientific/change assessment. Distinguish a justified invalid test from a valid unfavorable result.

Document planned versus actual execution, complete results, deviations and investigations, statistical limitations, each criterion's disposition, method changes and revalidation needs, receiving-lab capability, restrictions, and approval. EU Chapter 6 6.40 requires transfer-protocol deviations to be investigated before closure. For validation/release use, establish relevant method readiness before testing supports the decision; other tasks may run in parallel only when their prerequisites are satisfied.

Monitor initial routine performance as justified: suitability failures, OOS/OOT, standard/reagent changes, analyst and instrument effects. Reassess after relevant method, software, column, supplier, matrix, specification or laboratory changes; not every change requires full retransferring.

## FDA analytical-transfer supplement — reviewed 2026-10-08
FDA's *Analytical Procedures and Methods Validation for Drugs and Biologics* remains listed as Final, July 2015. Section VIII.B.2 (printed page 12) describes preapproved protocols and predetermined criteria, representative common samples, interlaboratory accuracy and precision, and inclusion of degraded or pertinent impurity samples for stability-indicating methods. Apply sample suitability and scientifically justified criteria rather than copying a default sample number. Method transfer, method replacement and product comparability are separate decisions. Section VIII.C addresses postmarketing changes; actual 21 CFR 314.70/601.12 applicability requires a project-specific review.

Section I does not directly address IND method validation and excludes specific bioassay/immunochemical validation recommendations. Do not use this guidance as a complete bioassay-transfer specification. Historical Q2(R1) citations do not displace applicable Q2(R2)/Q14 or regional implementation requirements. USP <1224> is an additional routing reference; its current official text/status was not verified by this supplement.
