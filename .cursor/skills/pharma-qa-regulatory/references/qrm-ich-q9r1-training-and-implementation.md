## Current implementation-status rule

The final Q9(R1) guideline was adopted at Step 4 on 18 January 2023. The current ICH-hosted text checked on 2026-09-29 records minor typographical corrections dated 26 January 2023 and 15 January 2025.

The ICH Q9(R1) revision was accompanied by implementation work on official training material. The 2023 IWG Concept Paper expressly identified conflicts between the legacy 2006-2010 Q9/Q8-Q10 training material and Q9(R1). ICH work plans subsequently recorded replacement or updating of legacy presentations, including a new introductory Q9(R1) presentation and updated Q8/Q9/Q10 material. The November 2025 Assembly record stated that the Training Group expected to complete the remaining briefing-pack work and submit it for publication by the end of December 2025.

At the 2026-09-29 public checkpoint, do **not** assume that every historical Q9 slide deck found online is current merely because it is hosted on an ICH domain. Before using a training slide as authority:
1. identify its date and status;
2. confirm it is Q9(R1)-aligned or explicitly retained after the R1 update;
3. reconcile any conflict against the current Step 4 guideline;
4. label training examples as implementation support rather than regulatory text.

## Six Q9(R1) implementation topics to test

Use the following six-topic screen when reviewing a QRM system or training package.

### 1. Subjectivity in QRM

Check whether the process manages subjectivity in hazard identification, probability/severity estimation, evaluation of controls and acceptance decisions. Use relevant data and knowledge, define assumptions, challenge bias, and increase independent challenge when uncertainty or importance warrants it.

### 2. Product availability risks

Include patient harm that can arise when quality/manufacturing problems affect product availability. Do not turn supply continuity into a justification for reduced GMP or product-quality standards.

### 3. Formality in QRM

Treat formality as a continuum rather than a binary formal/informal choice. Base the degree of rigor on uncertainty, importance and complexity, while keeping the level of effort and documentation commensurate with risk. Resource pressure is not a valid reason to under-formalize a material risk decision.

### 4. Risk-based decision-making

Make the decision question, knowledge base, alternatives, risk implications, controls, residual risk and review plan traceable. Recognize that QRM can support structured and less-structured decisions; a numerical score is not required for every decision.

### 5. Hazard identification

Use `Hazard Identification` for the Q9(R1) risk-assessment step. Distinguish the hazard, potential harm, failure/event pathway and associated risk. Do not rewrite legacy records solely to change terminology unless another reason requires revision, but use the R1 terminology for new work.

### 6. Risk review

Treat QRM as a lifecycle process. Use new knowledge, deviations, complaints, audits, changes, validation/CPV data, stability trends, supplier performance, inspections and other planned/unplanned events to revisit assumptions, controls and acceptance decisions.

## Legacy-material reconciliation rules

When an older Q9 or Q8/Q9/Q10 training document conflicts with Q9(R1), apply these corrections:

| Legacy concept | Q9(R1)-aligned treatment |
| --- | --- |
| `Risk Identification` as the named Q9 risk-assessment step | Use `Hazard Identification` for new Q9(R1) work. |
| Formal versus informal QRM as a binary choice | Use a continuum of formality based on uncertainty, importance and complexity. |
| A formal stand-alone risk document is always required | Scale documentation to the risk and formality; lower-formality QRM can be embedded in another PQS process. |
| Risk acceptance must always be a separately documented formal decision | Keep the acceptance basis traceable and appropriate to risk; do not impose a stand-alone form unless the PQS or case requires it. |
| Detectability is an independent factor in every QRM model | Detectability is method-dependent; detection controls can reduce the probability of harm but should not mechanically offset severe hazards. |
| QRM is mainly a manufacturing/GMP tool | Apply QRM across development, manufacturing, distribution, inspection and submission/review activities throughout the lifecycle. |
| Historical absence of events proves low risk | Treat event history as one evidence source and assess exposure, controls, data quality, uncertainty and plausible failure pathways. |

## Training-package QA checklist

Before approving internal QRM training, verify that it:
- uses the current Q9(R1) terminology and current Step 4 source;
- explains hazard, harm and risk as different concepts;
- teaches the formality continuum and the role of uncertainty, importance and complexity;
- teaches management/minimization of subjectivity rather than claiming subjectivity can be eliminated;
- shows that RPN, 1-10 scales, heat maps and fixed thresholds are optional internal methods, not ICH requirements;
- explains that detectability is not mandatory as a discrete factor in every method;
- covers product-availability patient risk without lowering GMP expectations;
- covers risk review and lifecycle feedback;
- distinguishes regulatory text from examples and professional practice;
- identifies the jurisdiction before stating that Q9(R1) is legally implemented.

## Output rule

When an answer relies on ICH training material, identify whether the point comes from the current Q9(R1) guideline, an official implementation/training document, a regional implementation document, or professional interpretation. Never use an old training slide to weaken or contradict Q9(R1).
