## 1. Purpose, scope, and evidence status

Use this reference for pharmaceutical product complaints, product quality complaints, suspected quality defects, complaint investigations, safety/pharmacovigilance triage, regulatory reporting, field alerts, biological product deviations, distribution complaints, complaint-to-recall trigger screening, complaint trending, and complaint-related PQR/APR review in China, the United States, and the European Union. Use [complaint-decision-matrices.md](complaint-decision-matrices.md) for case-level complaint triage, internal classification, investigation depth, regulatory clocks, MAH/contract-manufacturer interfaces, complaint-sample strategy, SOP design, and worked examples. When the task moves from screening into formal recall classification, strategy, execution, effectiveness, reconciliation, or termination, load [recall-regulatory-map.md](recall-regulatory-map.md) and [recall-decision-framework.md](recall-decision-framework.md).

Complaint-specific regulatory sources in this file were rechecked against official or government sources on **2026-09-18**. Recall-specific legal sources are maintained separately and were rechecked on **2026-09-24** in [recall-regulatory-map.md](recall-regulatory-map.md). This is a dated verification checkpoint, not a perpetual currentness guarantee. Revalidate the current official text whenever the task asks for current, effective, superseded, latest, or exact reporting requirements.

Do not treat every complaint as a confirmed quality defect. Do not treat every quality defect as a recall. Do not wait for the final root-cause investigation before taking time-critical patient-protection, regulatory-reporting, or recall actions when a separate trigger has already been met.

For product-specific or market-specific work, also load the applicable pharmacovigilance, registration, manufacturing, distribution, clinical-trial, biologic, controlled-substance, combination-product, or local competent-authority requirements. This reference is a core routing map, not an exhaustive substitute for all product-specific law.

## 2. Terminology and regulatory hierarchy

Use these terms consistently unless the governing procedure defines them differently:

- `Complaint / 投诉`: an allegation, report, or communication concerning a marketed or supplied product or related service. Determine whether it contains a product-quality, distribution, safety, falsification, or other issue.
- `Product Quality Complaint (PQC) / 产品质量投诉`: use when the complaint concerns identity, strength, quality, purity, packaging, labeling, integrity, performance, contamination, deterioration, defect, or other product-quality attributes. Do not assume this exact term is defined identically in all jurisdictions.
- `Quality defect / 质量缺陷`: a confirmed or suspected defect that can affect product quality, safety, efficacy, compliance, or marketability. Apply the jurisdiction's own terminology and notification rules.
- `Adverse event/adverse drug experience / 不良事件/药品不良反应相关信息`: a safety signal that may coexist with a quality complaint. Route to pharmacovigilance in parallel when applicable.
- `Recall / 召回`: retrieval or correction of distributed product under the applicable jurisdictional framework. Distinguish voluntary, authority-requested, authority-ordered, and other market actions where applicable.
- `Distribution complaint / 配送或流通投诉`: a complaint primarily concerning storage, transport, delivery, supply-chain handling, or distribution service. It can still reveal a product quality defect and must be escalated when that possibility exists.

Regulatory hierarchy matters:

- China: distinguish laws, administrative regulations, departmental rules/GMP, NMPA normative documents, technical guidance, and company procedures.
- United States: distinguish binding CFR requirements from FDA guidance, compliance-policy documents, inspectional materials, warning letters, and industry practice.
- European Union: distinguish Union legislation and national law from EudraLex GMP/GDP guidance, EMA/HMA Union procedures, GVP guidance, and national competent-authority procedures.

When a source is guidance rather than binding legislation, identify it as guidance and do not restate it as a statutory requirement.

## 3. Cross-jurisdiction complaint workflow

Use the following workflow as a professional synthesis. Tie jurisdiction-specific requirements to the source map below.

### 3.1 Intake and evidence preservation

1. Record the original complaint promptly and preserve the complainant's wording.
2. Capture available product identification, strength, dosage form, pack, lot/batch, expiry, market/country, purchase/distribution channel, dates, event description, contact details where lawful, sample/photo availability, and immediate patient/product status.
3. Preserve complaint samples, photos, shipping materials, returned units, electronic records, call logs, and other relevant evidence with traceability and data-integrity controls.
4. Record source and receipt date/time because regulatory reporting clocks may run from receipt, discovery, or knowledge rather than investigation completion.

### 3.2 Parallel triage

Screen without delay for all applicable branches; more than one branch can apply:

- potential product quality defect;
- patient safety/adverse event or lack-of-efficacy information;
- falsification/tampering/counterfeit concern;
- temperature excursion, transport, storage, or distribution issue;
- labeling, mix-up, contamination, deterioration, specification failure, or packaging-integrity issue;
- potential regulatory-reporting trigger;
- potential recall, market action, stop-ship, quarantine, or other interim risk control.

Do not require the quality investigation to finish before forwarding reportable safety information to pharmacovigilance or before assessing a separate quality-defect/FAR/BPDR/recall reporting obligation.

### 3.3 Initial quality and health-risk assessment

Document:

- allegation and defect mechanism;
- severity and plausible patient/quality impact;
- probability and detectability where useful;
- affected/suspected batch, market, channel, population, and use conditions;
- distributed versus undistributed status;
- need for immediate quarantine, stop shipment, customer communication, medical advice, regulatory contact, or recall assessment;
- uncertainty and missing evidence.

Use QRM proportionately. A numerical FMEA score is not required for every complaint and must not replace clinical/quality judgment or explicit regulatory triggers.

### 3.4 Investigation decision and scope

1. Determine whether an investigation is required under the applicable framework.
2. If the framework permits a decision not to investigate, document the scientific/regulatory rationale and responsible decision-maker.
3. Investigate manufacturing, packaging, labeling, testing, release, storage, transport, distribution, complaint sample, retain/reference samples, deviations, changes, maintenance, environmental data, supplier data, stability, and prior complaints as relevant.
4. Extend the assessment to other batches, products, components, markets, or time periods when a shared cause or common exposure is plausible or the regulation requires extension.
5. Distinguish complaint validity from defect existence: inability to reproduce a complaint does not automatically prove there was no quality defect.

### 3.5 Root cause, CAPA, risk control, and closure

- Determine root and contributing causes using evidence; avoid unsupported `human error` conclusions.
- Define corrections, corrective action, preventive/systemic action, owner, due date, effectiveness check, and residual risk as appropriate.
- Reassess recall/regulatory-reporting decisions as new evidence appears.
- Respond to the complainant when appropriate and permitted, without disclosing unsupported conclusions or confidential investigation information.
- Trend complaint categories, batches, products, causes, recurrence, markets, severity, and closure performance to identify emerging signals.
- Feed relevant trends into PQR/APR, management review, supplier oversight, change control, training, validation, and continuous improvement.

Internal target timelines can be defined by the company, but they must not obscure or extend an external statutory/regulatory reporting deadline.

## 4. China requirements and source map

### 4.1 China GMP: complaint, investigation, PQR, and recall linkage

Use *Good Manufacturing Practice for Drugs (2010 Revision)* as the core GMP source and verify the current official text before clause-level conclusions. Key routing points include:

- Articles 252-253: CAPA/investigation system includes complaints and recalls among inputs requiring investigation/analysis and corrective/preventive action as applicable.
- Article 266: PQR includes quality-related returns, complaints, recalls, and their investigations.
- Articles 269-270: adverse-drug-reaction monitoring/reporting responsibilities create a safety-information interface.
- Article 271: establish procedures for complaint registration, evaluation, investigation, and handling; when a complaint may involve a product defect, consider whether recall is needed.
- Articles 272-276: assign competent personnel; communicate all complaint/investigation information to the Quality Authorized Person under Article 272; register and review complaints; investigate quality-defect complaints; assess other batches when a batch defect is found or suspected; maintain records; periodically review complaint data for trends, recurrence, and possible recall signals.
- Article 277: for production error, deterioration, or other major quality problem, take appropriate measures and report to the regulator where required.
- Article 290: in contract manufacturing, the contract giver/MAH must be able to access records relevant to complaints, suspected defects, and recalls as applicable.
- Articles 293-305: establish and maintain a recall system, distribution traceability, recall procedures, responsible personnel, prompt execution, records, reconciliation, final reporting, and periodic effectiveness assessment.

Use these clauses together rather than reviewing a complaint SOP only against Article 271.

### 4.2 Drug Recall Administrative Measures, NMPA Announcement No. 92 of 2022

Use the current official text of *药品召回管理办法* as the core China recall source. At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, the 2022 measures were effective from 2022-11-01.

Key routing requirements include:

- MAH responsibility for establishing the recall system; collecting quality/safety information; investigating and evaluating potential quality issues; and implementing recall when required.
- Manufacturers, distributors, and medical institutions should communicate discovered quality/safety problems to the MAH and take/report risk-control actions as applicable.
- Investigation/evaluation should consider adverse reactions/events, formulation/process/specifications, GMP, storage/transport, use conditions, affected batches, quantities, and distribution.
- Risk assessment should consider probability of harm, affected populations including vulnerable populations, severity/urgency, and consequences.
- Recall classification uses Level I, II, and III based on health-harm seriousness under the measures.
- Once a recall decision is made, apply the explicit notification, progress-reporting, completion-reporting, record-retention, and effectiveness requirements in the current text.

For exact numeric timelines, use Section 8 below and recheck the current official source before issuing a controlled procedure.

### 4.3 China pharmacovigilance interface

Use the current *Good Pharmacovigilance Practice / 药物警戒质量管理规范*, NMPA Announcement No. 65 of 2021, for safety-information collection, evaluation, reporting, and PV-system responsibilities. At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, the government source identified the GVP as currently effective.

For complaint workflows:

- screen each complaint for safety information, including harmful reactions potentially caused by a product-quality issue;
- transfer applicable information to pharmacovigilance promptly under the company's interface procedure;
- permit parallel quality and PV investigations;
- reconcile final quality conclusions with safety assessment when relevant;
- for valid individual case reports within scope, the current GVP provides that serious adverse reactions are reported as soon as possible and no later than **15 days** after the MAH first becomes aware of a report meeting the minimum reporting requirements, and non-serious adverse reactions no later than **30 days**;
- the current GVP also contains a **24-hour** route when a foreign drug regulator requires suspension of sale/use or market withdrawal because of an adverse reaction; verify the exact trigger, recipient, and current wording before controlled use;
- do not turn these specific PV timelines into a universal timeline for all complaints.

Additional urgent safety routes: under Ministry of Health Order 81 Articles 21–22, death cases require immediate reporting and the manufacturer's death-case investigation report is due within 15 days. Articles 27–29 require immediate group-event reporting to the stated local authorities/monitoring body and the manufacturer's investigation report within 7 days. Map the relevant legal actor, PV interface and reporting destination; do not replace these with a generic 15/30-day ICSR or complaint target. Current SAMR text inspected 2026-10-08; see the targeted review.

### 4.4 China GSP and distribution scope

When the actor is a drug distributor, retail entity, or distribution-service provider, also load the current *Good Supply Practice for Pharmaceutical Products / 药品经营质量管理规范 (GSP)*. At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, the SAMR current rule text includes:

- Article 114: the enterprise shall establish a complaint-management operating procedure covering complaint channels/methods, records, investigation and evaluation, handling measures, feedback, and follow-up;
- Articles 115-116: assign personnel for after-sales complaint management; identify causes of quality complaints, take effective measures, provide timely handling/feedback, notify suppliers/manufacturers when necessary, and record complaint/handling information for query and follow-up;
- Articles 117-119: for serious quality problems in sold drugs, take the specified stop-sale/recovery/reporting actions; assist manufacturer recall; and perform adverse-reaction monitoring/reporting;
- for retail scope, Articles 174-177 address quality complaint handling, adverse-reaction information, recovery of sold drugs with serious quality problems, and recall assistance.

Distinguish a pure service/distribution complaint from a complaint that may reveal product quality, storage, transport, falsification, safety, or recall risk. Apply the actor-specific GSP chapter and current text.

### 4.5 China MAH governance

When the MAH uses a contract manufacturer or third-party logistics provider, do not transfer the MAH's statutory quality-and-safety responsibility through contract wording. Ensure complaint access, investigation cooperation, sample/evidence access, change/deviation communication, regulatory escalation, recall execution, distribution traceability, and data availability are addressed in the quality agreement or other controlled agreement.

Also use the current *Provisions on the Supervision and Administration of Drug Marketing Authorization Holders' Implementation of Primary Responsibility for Drug Quality and Safety*, NMPA Announcement No. 126 of 2022, effective 2023-03-01. At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, relevant provisions included:

- Article 17: MAH oversight of entrusted storage, transportation, and sales, including capability/risk evaluation, agreements, and periodic review of entrusted storage/transport;
- Article 19: MAH recall system and action when quality problems or other safety hazards are identified, including investigation/evaluation, recall plan/notice filing and completion reporting under the applicable recall rules;
- Article 20: MAH pharmacovigilance system;
- Article 21: post-marketing risk-management/evaluation and risk-control measures including production/sales suspension and recall where justified;
- Article 22: immediate effective controls when a major safety event related to drug quality occurs;
- Article 26: periodic quality-risk review by the Quality Head, including recurring and emerging risks.

Use these as governance links around complaint management; do not state that the document creates a standalone complaint procedure if the requirement arises through GMP, PV, recall, distribution, or other systems.

## 5. United States requirements and source map

### 5.1 21 CFR 211.198 - Complaint files

For finished drug products subject to Part 211, use the current eCFR text of **21 CFR 211.198**. Core requirements include:

- written procedures for handling **all written and oral complaints** concerning a drug product;
- Quality Control Unit review of complaints involving a possible failure to meet specifications;
- determination whether an investigation under 21 CFR 211.192 is necessary;
- written complaint records with required product/complaint information;
- investigation findings and follow-up when an investigation is performed;
- documented reason and responsible person when no investigation is performed;
- complaint records should include the drug product name/strength, lot number, complainant name, nature of complaint, and reply to complainant, plus investigation findings/follow-up when an investigation is performed;
- if no investigation is performed, document the reason and the name of the responsible person;
- under the current paragraph (b), retain complaint records until at least **1 year after expiration of the drug product or 1 year after complaint receipt, whichever is longer**; for certain OTC drug products exempt from expiration dating, the current rule uses **3 years after distribution**.

Do not reduce the US procedure to written complaints only; the CFR expressly includes oral complaints. Recheck the current eCFR before copying exact retention language into a controlled SOP.

### 5.2 21 CFR 211.192 - Investigation extension

Use **21 CFR 211.192** with 211.198. A required investigation must be thorough and, where applicable, extend to:

- other batches of the same drug product; and
- other drug products that may have been associated with the specific failure or discrepancy.

Document conclusions and follow-up.

### 5.3 21 CFR 211.180(e)-(f) - Annual review and management notification

Use **21 CFR 211.180(e)** for at-least-annual evaluation of the quality standards of each drug product. The review includes complaints, recalls, returned/salvaged drug products, and investigations conducted under 211.192.

Use **21 CFR 211.180(f)** for written notification of responsible officials concerning relevant investigations, recalls, FDA observations, or regulatory actions as specified by the provision.

### 5.4 21 CFR 314.81(b)(1) - Field Alert Reports for approved NDAs/ANDAs

For products covered by approved NDAs and, through 21 CFR 314.98, ANDAs, assess every relevant quality complaint or internal finding against the **Field Alert Report (FAR)** triggers in 21 CFR 314.81(b)(1).

The rule covers specified information concerning distributed drug products, including certain:

- mix-ups or misapplication of labeling;
- bacteriological contamination;
- significant chemical, physical, or other change or deterioration; and
- failure of a distributed batch to meet the specifications established for it in the application.

When the trigger is met, the applicant's reporting clock is **within 3 working days of receipt of the information by the applicant**, subject to the current rule and FDA implementation. Do not wait for investigation completion or confirmed root cause before assessing/submitting a FAR.

FDA's Final July 2021 FAR Q&A III.3(a) (printed page 6) interprets day 0 to include qualifying information identified at a contractor (example: contract laboratory learns of a sterility failure). Working days are Monday–Friday excluding US Federal holidays. Preserve the CFR applicant-receipt wording as regulatory text, but apply this FDA interpretation to operational clocks; delayed forwarding to headquarters must not restart the window. Q&A III.1(d) expects relevant customer complaints to be assessed within the same 3-working-day window, with FAR submitted within it if triggered. See the 2026-10-08 targeted review for source status.

Use FDA's current FAR page and current FAR guidance for operational questions, but distinguish guidance from the binding CFR.

### 5.5 US pharmacovigilance interface

For approved NDA/ANDA products, assess complaint safety information under the current **21 CFR 314.80** framework. For licensed biological products, assess under the current **21 CFR 600.80** framework and other product-specific provisions as applicable.

At the 2026-09-18 eCFR checkpoint, the applicable 15-day routes require serious and unexpected postmarketing adverse experiences to be submitted as soon as possible and no later than **15 calendar days** after initial receipt by the applicant/licensee, subject to the product and reportability criteria in the current rule. Do not generalize this deadline to every complaint or every adverse event.

A complaint can generate both a product-quality investigation and an adverse-experience report. Run these tracks in parallel and reconcile them at closure.

Do not rely on the cross-references contained in 21 CFR 211.198 alone as a complete modern pharmacovigilance map; load the applicable current safety-reporting rule for the product.

### 5.6 Biological Product Deviation Reports

For licensed biological products, assess applicable manufacturing/deviation information under **21 CFR 600.14** and current FDA Biological Product Deviation reporting instructions.

The current rule requires reporting of qualifying events associated with distributed product under the stated criteria and, when reportable, generally requires submission as soon as possible and no later than **45 calendar days from discovery of information reasonably suggesting a reportable event**. Verify product and establishment applicability before applying this route.

A complaint itself is not automatically a BPDR; the underlying event must meet the rule's criteria.

### 5.7 US recall framework

Use **21 CFR Part 7, Subpart C** for FDA recall policy, procedures, health-hazard evaluation, classification, recall strategy, effectiveness checks, firm-initiated recall, and related responsibilities. Label Part 7 Subpart C accurately as FDA's recall policy/procedure framework rather than converting every provision into a universal mandatory reporting statute.

For a complaint that indicates a potentially violative distributed product:

- assess health hazard and affected distribution;
- determine whether voluntary recall or other market action is appropriate;
- make any required or appropriate FDA communications promptly;
- maintain distribution, notification, effectiveness, reconciliation, and disposition evidence.

Product-specific statutory authorities can impose additional recall or reporting duties; load them when applicable.

### 5.8 API scope in the United States

Do not automatically apply 21 CFR 211.198 as the legal complaint provision to an API merely because the API supports a US product. For API complaint/recall controls, load the applicable statutory/regulatory framework plus current FDA/ICH Q7 implementation and clearly distinguish guidance from binding requirements.

### 5.9 US contract manufacturing and quality agreements

Use FDA's final guidance *Contract Manufacturing Arrangements for Drugs: Quality Agreements* (November 2016) as nonbinding current-thinking guidance when complaint responsibilities cross organizations. It supports clear allocation and documentation of CGMP manufacturing activities, but does not replace either party's applicable legal/CGMP responsibilities.

For complaint-system design, define rapid information exchange, original complaint preservation, site investigation, access to records/samples, product-owner/applicant oversight, regulatory reporting support, recall execution, CAPA, and dispute/escalation routes. For licensed biologics, also assess product-specific FDA guidance and BPDR responsibilities.

For APIs, FDA's current Q7 guidance Section 15 provides a useful complaint/recall framework: quality-related complaints, whether oral or written, should be recorded and investigated under a written procedure; records should support trend/frequency/severity review; and recall procedures should define evaluation, initiation, notification, and recalled-material handling. Treat Q7 as guidance/implemented GMP framework, not 21 CFR 211.198.

## 6. European Union requirements and source map

### 6.1 Commission Directive (EU) 2017/1572 - legal GMP basis

Use the current consolidated/legal text of **Commission Directive (EU) 2017/1572**, particularly Article 13 for complaints and product recall, as part of the legal GMP basis for human medicinal products within its scope.

The framework requires a system for recording and reviewing complaints together with an effective system for prompt recall, and requires investigation/communication of relevant product defects under the applicable legal and national arrangements.

Do not use EU GMP Chapter 8 as the sole legal source when a task asks for the legal basis.

### 6.2 EU GMP Part I, Chapter 8 - Complaints, Quality Defects and Product Recalls

Use the current official **EudraLex Volume 4, Part I, Chapter 8** for operational GMP expectations. At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, the Commission index listed the chapter as in operation since 1 March 2015.

Core controls include:

- documented procedures to record, assess, investigate, and review complaints including potential quality defects;
- prompt and effective recall capability;
- QRM application to quality-defect investigation and recall/risk-reduction decisions;
- adequate trained personnel and resources, with roles independent of sales/marketing unless justified;
- documentation and assessment of all complaints for potential quality defect or other issue;
- consideration of falsification;
- routing of non-quality complaints, including suspected adverse events, to the appropriate function;
- support for adverse-event investigation through batch/product quality investigation where relevant;
- investigation of defect description, extent, samples, batch records/certification, distribution, risk, recall/mitigation, supply impact, regulatory communication, root cause, CAPA, and effectiveness;
- evaluation of other batches/products and previous complaints/trends where relevant;
- timely competent-authority communication for quality defects that may result in recall or abnormal restriction in supply;
- root-cause and CAPA expectations, including avoidance of superficial `human error` conclusions;
- recall procedures capable of prompt execution and, where necessary, operation at any time;
- traceable distribution records, reconciliation, effectiveness assessment, and consideration of shortage/supply impact.

Chapter 8 does not create a single universal numeric complaint-investigation closure deadline. Do not invent one.

### 6.3 Directive 2001/83/EC: withdrawal, quality-defect systems, and market actions

For medicinal products within scope, inspect the current consolidated **Directive 2001/83/EC**, including Articles 117, 117a, and 123 where relevant.

Use these provisions to evaluate:

- authority withdrawal/prohibition grounds;
- member-state systems for suspected quality defects and suspected falsified medicines;
- rapid-alert/recall system expectations;
- MAH notifications concerning certain suspension/withdrawal or market-action decisions.

Do not misstate the Article 117a urgent public-announcement timing as a universal MAH complaint-reporting deadline; it is part of the member-state/authority system and must be interpreted in context.

### 6.4 EMA/HMA quality-defect and recall procedures

For centrally authorised medicinal products, use EMA's current **Quality defects and recalls** page and the current **Compilation of Union Procedures on Inspections and Exchange of Information**.

Operationally:

- determine whether the defect is suspected or confirmed;
- assess patient risk, affected batches/markets, supply impact, root cause, CAPA, and recall/other risk reduction;
- identify the correct reporting route: EMA for centrally authorised products as applicable, or the relevant national competent authority for nationally authorised products;
- follow the current EMA GMP/GDP Q&A principle that suspected quality defects should be reported to the competent authority responsible for the manufacturing site/importer and to the competent authority in each supplied EEA market, with EMA also notified for centrally authorised products when applicable;
- do not wait for defect confirmation or completion of the investigation: reporting should be initiated when available information supports detection of the issue and the initial risk assessment indicates that it could result in market action;
- coordinate notification before market action unless immediate action is required to protect patient/animal health;
- use the current Union quality-defect procedure/classification document at execution time because versions, forms, and national routes can change. At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, EMA's Compilation of Union Procedures listed a revised quality-defect management/classification procedure updated in 2026.

Do not generalize a single EMA reporting route to all EU/EEA products.

### 6.5 EU GDP distribution complaints

For wholesale distribution, use the current **Guidelines of 5 November 2013 on Good Distribution Practice of medicinal products for human use (2013/C 343/01)**, especially Chapter 6.

Key controls include:

- written procedures and records for complaints, returns, suspected falsified medicines, and recalls;
- distinction between complaints about product quality and complaints about distribution;
- prompt communication of product-quality or potential-defect complaints to the manufacturer and/or MAH as applicable;
- thorough investigation of distribution complaints;
- responsible personnel and adequate support;
- CAPA/follow-up and competent-authority notification as applicable.

A distribution complaint can escalate into a product quality defect; do not close it as a service issue without appropriate assessment.

### 6.6 EU pharmacovigilance interface

Use the current EMA GVP framework, including **GVP Module VI** as applicable, for collection, management, and submission of suspected adverse-reaction information.

Complaint SOPs should require safety screening and prompt routing to pharmacovigilance. Quality and PV investigations can run in parallel. Under Directive 2001/83/EC Article 107(3) and the current Module VI framework, serious valid ICSRs are submitted within **15 days** and non-serious valid ICSRs within **90 days**, subject to the current legal scope and submission modalities.

At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, EMA still listed GVP Module VI Rev. 2 while noting broader GVP updates associated with newer implementing changes and ICH work. Verify the current GVP revision, legal basis, and submission arrangements at execution time.

### 6.7 EU active substances/API

For active-substance complaints and recalls, load the current **EU GMP Part II** / regionally implemented ICH Q7 framework. Section 15 addresses quality-related complaints and recalls for APIs/intermediates. Do not apply the finished-product Chapter 8 framework mechanically when the actor/product scope is API-only.

### 6.8 EU investigational medicinal products

Chapter 8 can be relevant to investigational medicinal products, but regulatory reporting and recall routes differ from commercial-market procedures. For IMP complaints or defects, load the current Clinical Trials Regulation framework, applicable EU GMP/IMP requirements, sponsor procedures, and national competent-authority requirements before assigning reporting paths or deadlines.

## 8. Timelines and retention checkpoints

Always verify exact current wording before inserting a mandatory timeline into a controlled SOP.

### China pharmacovigilance and recall measures

At the 2026-09-18 checkpoint, the current China GVP provides the following complaint-relevant ICSR checkpoints when the report is valid and within scope:

- serious adverse reactions: as soon as possible and no later than **15 days** after the MAH first becomes aware of information meeting the minimum reporting requirements;
- non-serious adverse reactions: no later than **30 days**;
- certain foreign regulatory suspension/withdrawal actions because of adverse reactions: assess the **24-hour** notification route in the current GVP.

These are PV clocks, not complaint-investigation deadlines.

At the same checkpoint, *药品召回管理办法* (2022 No. 92) provides the following key recall-process timings after the relevant trigger/decision under the measures:

- recall notification after recall decision: Level I within **1 day**, Level II within **3 days**, Level III within **7 days**;
- recall progress reports: Level I **daily**, Level II **every 3 days**, Level III **every 7 days**;
- recall completion report: generally within **10 working days after completion of recall** as specified by the measures;
- recall/handling records: **5 years and not less than 1 year after the drug expiry date**, subject to the current official wording and scope.

Do not back-calculate these as a universal complaint-investigation deadline. A complaint can require action before a recall decision, and many complaints do not become recalls.

### United States

- **21 CFR 314.81(b)(1) FAR:** within **3 working days** of receipt of qualifying information by the applicant, when the provision applies.
- **21 CFR 314.80 / 600.80 serious and unexpected postmarketing adverse experiences:** generally as soon as possible and no later than **15 calendar days** after initial receipt by the applicant/licensee, subject to the current rule and product applicability.
- **21 CFR 600.14 qualifying biological product deviations:** as soon as possible, and no later than **45 calendar days** from discovery of information reasonably suggesting a reportable event, subject to applicability.
- **21 CFR 211.198 complaint records:** apply the exact retention rule in current paragraph (b), including the special rule for certain OTC products lacking expiration dating.

Do not wait for the complaint-investigation target date if a 3-working-day, 15-calendar-day, or other external clock is already running.

### European Union

EU GMP Chapter 8 and related legal/Union procedures commonly use obligations such as `timely`, `without delay`, `prompt`, or `forthwith` depending on the source and action. Do not convert these into an invented universal numeric complaint-reporting deadline.

For complaint-associated pharmacovigilance, the current Directive 2001/83/EC / GVP framework uses **15 days for serious valid ICSRs** and **90 days for non-serious valid ICSRs**, subject to the current legal scope and reporting arrangements. These are PV clocks, not quality-complaint closure targets.

For suspected quality defects that could result in market action, the current EMA GMP/GDP Q&A expects authority notification before market action unless urgent patient/animal-health protection requires immediate action, and it does not require completion of the investigation before reporting.

National competent authorities can impose forms, channels, local deadlines, and notification expectations. For a real defect report, identify the authorization route and reporting authority before stating the deadline.

## 9. Investigation depth and extent rules

For a reusable internal severity model, Level A/B/C investigation-depth model, evidence matrices, complaint-sample controls, MAH/contract-manufacturer interface, and worked scenarios, also read [complaint-decision-matrices.md](complaint-decision-matrices.md). Those internal models are professional controls and must not be presented as statutory recall classifications.

### Minimum decision questions

For each complaint, answer or document why not applicable:

1. Is the product/batch identifiable?
2. Is there a potential quality defect, safety issue, falsification issue, or distribution failure?
3. Is any external reporting clock triggered?
4. Is immediate containment or market action needed?
5. Is a complaint sample available and is it representative/traceable?
6. What manufacturing/testing/packaging/distribution evidence is relevant?
7. Could other batches, products, components, markets, sites, suppliers, or time periods share the cause?
8. Has the same or similar complaint occurred previously?
9. Is root cause supported by evidence?
10. Are CAPA and effectiveness checks proportionate?
11. Has recall/field-alert/quality-defect/PV assessment been updated as evidence evolved?
12. Is closure supported by objective evidence and all interfaces reconciled?

### Do not use weak closure logic

Avoid conclusions such as:

- `No defect found in retain sample; therefore complaint invalid.`
- `Only one complaint was received; therefore no systemic issue.`
- `Operator error; retrain.` without evidence of the causal mechanism and system contributors.
- `Product met release specifications; therefore no investigation needed.`
- `The complaint could not be reproduced; therefore no regulatory reporting evaluation is required.`

Use the weight of evidence, product/market risk, recurrence, detectability, and the governing regulatory trigger.

## 10. SOP/SMP review checklist

When drafting or auditing a complaint procedure, verify that it addresses, as applicable:

### Governance and scope

- products, markets, lifecycle stages, and organizations covered;
- MAH/applicant/license holder/manufacturer/distributor/CRO or service-provider roles;
- Quality Unit authority and escalation;
- pharmacovigilance, medical, regulatory affairs, manufacturing, supply chain, and legal interfaces;
- quality agreements and third-party complaint-forwarding timelines.

### Intake and records

- all complaint channels, including oral/electronic complaints where required;
- original allegation preserved;
- batch/product traceability;
- date/time of receipt and date/time of internal forwarding;
- complaint sample/photo/return handling;
- privacy/confidentiality and data-integrity controls.

### Triage and regulatory clocks

- quality vs non-quality/distribution classification;
- safety/PV screening;
- falsification/tampering screening;
- FAR assessment for applicable US NDA/ANDA products;
- BPDR assessment for applicable US biologics;
- EU quality-defect notification route and authority selection;
- China recall assessment;
- stop-ship/quarantine/market-action criteria;
- requirement that external reporting timelines override internal complaint-closure targets.

### Investigation

- investigation criteria and documented no-investigation rationale where legally permissible;
- related-batch/product extension;
- batch record, testing, retain/reference sample, stability, supplier, storage/transport, deviation/change, maintenance, and distribution review as relevant;
- complaint-sample examination strategy and limitations;
- root-cause standards;
- risk assessment and uncertainty;
- repeat/recurrence and trend review.

### CAPA, response, and closure

- corrections and CAPA linked to evidence;
- effectiveness checks;
- complainant response control;
- regulatory/PV/recall reconciliation before closure;
- PQR/APR/trending feed;
- management escalation for significant or recurring issues;
- record retention consistent with each market's requirement.

### Recall linkage

- recall decision authority;
- distribution traceability;
- recall classification where applicable;
- authority communication and forms;
- consignee/customer communication;
- effectiveness checks;
- reconciliation and returned-product disposition;
- completion/final reporting;
- recall-system effectiveness review.

## 11. Output rules for regulatory analyses

For complaint tasks, structure the answer as:

1. `Regulatory text/evidence`: jurisdiction, source, clause/section, exact trigger, and currentness status.
2. `Regulatory interpretation`: explain how the trigger applies to the fact pattern; identify assumptions and uncertainty.
3. `Professional assessment`: recommended risk-based controls, workflow design, or SOP wording beyond explicit regulation.

For cross-jurisdiction comparisons, compare by substantive topic rather than merely aligning clause numbers:

- intake and oral/written scope;
- Quality Unit/MAH responsibility;
- investigation trigger;
- investigation extension;
- safety/PV interface;
- regulator notification;
- FAR/BPDR or EU quality-defect route;
- recall decision/communication;
- trending/PQR/APR;
- records/retention;
- third-party/contract-manufacturing interface.

Use `not identified in reviewed sources` rather than `no requirement` unless the search was sufficient to support absence.

When drafting a procedure, do not silently turn company target timelines into legal requirements. Label internal targets as company controls and preserve shorter external regulatory clocks.
