## Purpose and activation

Use this reference for questions involving intermediate-product hold time, in-process hold time, bulk-product hold time, maximum batch manufacturing time, temporary storage between unit operations, pre-packaging storage, hold-time validation, or extension/change of an established hold time or storage condition.

This file is a jurisdictional routing and interpretation map. It does not convert guidance into law. For current requirements, re-open the relevant official source and confirm the product type, lifecycle stage, market, and applicable dossier commitments before making a compliance conclusion.

Topic-specific currentness checkpoint: **2026-09-21**.

## Scope guardrails

Always establish which material and stage are being discussed before applying a rule:

- finished-dosage-form intermediate, e.g. blend, granules, tablet cores, coating solution, coated tablets awaiting primary packaging;
- bulk drug product that has completed processing except final packaging;
- sterile bulk or aseptically processed solution;
- API intermediate under ICH Q7 / PIC/S Part II rather than a finished-dosage-form intermediate;
- investigational product, biologic, radiopharmaceutical, herbal product, or other special category;
- material stored within one manufacturing site versus transported between sites;
- routine commercial production versus development, validation, registration, transfer, or post-approval change.

Do not automatically apply a finished-dosage-form hold-time rule to API intermediates, or a chemical oral-solid rule to sterile or biological products.

## Terminology controls

Keep the following concepts separate:

| Term | Working meaning | Do not confuse with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hold time` | Established time during which a material/intermediate/bulk product may be held under specified conditions and remain suitable for the next step or within defined criteria | finished-product shelf life, API retest period, cleaning hold time |
| `intermediate product` | Partly processed material that requires further processing | API intermediate unless the governing source is ICH Q7/PIC/S Part II |
| `bulk product` | Product that has completed processing up to, but not including, final packaging | finished product in market pack |
| `maximum batch manufacturing time` | Maximum elapsed time from a defined start of manufacture to completion of a defined downstream point, used by some regulatory frameworks as an alternative or complement to individual hold times | individual unit-operation processing time |
| `processing time limit` | Time limit for completion of a production phase or step | hold time between steps |
| `cumulative hold time` | Combined elapsed holds across multiple defined stages where total exposure may affect quality | one isolated hold point |

For China CDE 2025 No. 6, the controlled source defines intermediate product as a material generated during production that is not the final product and expressly treats product awaiting packaging as an intermediate for that guideline. Preserve that source-specific definition when applying the Chinese oral-solid guideline.

## China

### Controlled source

Bundled controlled copy:

`references/regulations/intermediate-hold-time/china-cde-2025-oral-solid-intermediate-hold-time.pdf`

Source identity: China CDE Announcement No. 6 of 2025, technical guideline on hold-time studies for intermediates of chemical oral solid dosage forms, issued January 2025. The controlled copy is evidence of the inspected text only; verify current status at the CDE source when currentness matters.

### Scope and trigger

Apply this guideline specifically to chemical oral solid dosage forms and temporary storage of intermediates during non-continuous manufacturing. Do not generalize its 30-day logic to sterile products, biological products, APIs, or all dosage forms without another applicable basis.

Key source-derived controls:

- If the total dosage-form manufacturing process normally exceeds 30 days, perform an intermediate-product hold-time study.
- If the total process does not exceed 30 days, use quality risk assessment to determine whether a hold-time study is needed.
- The registration applicant/manufacturer is the responsible party for the study strategy.
- Assess factors such as packaging/container, temperature, humidity, and the effect of the held material on later processing and final-product CQAs.
- Study at least at pilot scale. If the study is not at commercial scale, confirm the conclusion at commercial scale covering the intended site.
- At least one batch is used to establish the hold time under the guideline; use risk assessment to justify additional batches and representative strengths.
- Study duration covers the proposed maximum hold time and includes at least beginning, intermediate, and end time points.
- Select physical, chemical, and microbiological attributes that can change during the hold and may affect finished-product quality, safety, and/or efficacy.
- Use representative storage conditions and the same commercial temporary-storage container where possible; justify equivalent smaller containers and evaluate worst-case headspace where relevant.
- For cross-site transfer, include transfer time/conditions and, where necessary, use accelerated evidence to address short-term excursions.
- Establish the hold time from stability results and operational need; evaluate trends statistically where necessary.
- If assay decreases or degradants increase, consider finished-product accelerated/long-term stability using product made from the maximum-held intermediate and/or shorten the intermediate hold time.

### Example attributes from the controlled China guide

Use these as examples, not mandatory lists:

| Stage | Example study object | Example attributes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| processed API to dispensing | processed API | appearance, loss on drying/water, related substances, assay, particle size, crystal form |
| binder preparation to granulation | binder solution | appearance, viscosity if needed, microbial limits |
| final blending to compression | final blend/granules | appearance, water, blend uniformity, particle-size distribution, bulk/tapped density, related substances, assay, microbial limits |
| compression to coating | tablet cores | appearance, water, hardness, friability, disintegration, dissolution/release, related substances, assay, microbial limits |
| coating solution preparation to coating | coating solution | appearance, microbial limits |
| coated tablets to primary packaging | coated tablets | appearance, disintegration, dissolution/release, related substances, assay, water, microbial limits |

## European Union

### Source hierarchy

Use the current official EMA/EU sources in this order for this topic:

1. Directive 2001/83/EC and the current EU variation framework for legally binding dossier/variation obligations.
2. EudraLex Volume 4, including Part I and applicable annexes, for GMP expectations.
3. EMA/CHMP/QWP/245074/2015, *Guideline on manufacture of the finished dosage form - Revision 1*, for dossier-level hold-time expectations.
4. Current product-specific or dosage-form-specific guidance where applicable.

### EMA finished-dosage-form guideline

The current effective EMA guideline establishes the following route:

- Manufacturing durations of critical steps and hold times should be stated and justified where product-specific relevance exists.
- Intermediate hold-time validation is a GMP matter and normally does not need routine presentation in the marketing-authorisation application.
- Certain products, including sterile and biological products, may require relevant hold-time data in the dossier.
- State whether storage is required before final packaging and the temperature, humidity, or other environmental conditions.
- Where relevant, state, justify, and support with data the maximum bulk hold time or the maximum batch manufacturing time from start of manufacture to completion of primary packaging.
- Supporting evidence may come from challenging the maximum hold time in process-validation studies or from dedicated bulk-stability studies.
- Minimise processing/holding time and justify the proposed limits.
- As a general rule in this guideline, prolonged storage is >30 days for solid oral dosage forms and >24 h for sterile products.
- Where relevant, support the hold time with stability data from at least two pilot-scale batches at representative temperature and humidity.
- If bulk/intermediate is transported between sites, evaluate transport conditions and short/long excursions; justify the container-closure system.

### Current EU variation route

Under the current 2025 EU variation guideline, Q.II.b.3(e), a change in hold time and/or storage conditions of an intermediate or bulk product used in finished-product manufacture is classified as Type IB in the stated route. Documentation includes data validating the proposed change using a minimum of two pilot- or commercial-scale batches, description/specification of the intermediate/bulk container, a commercial-scale verification commitment when pilot data are used, and confirmation/justification of the finished-product shelf-life basis.

Do not apply this route mechanically to every product. Confirm product type, marketing-authorisation route, the exact approved dossier commitment, and current variation classification before filing.

### EU GMP / Annex 15 / sterile Annex 1

- Use current Annex 15 for lifecycle qualification/validation, QRM, process validation, transportation verification, and change control supporting the hold-time strategy.
- At the 2026-09-21 checkpoint, the operative EU Annex 15 remains the 2015 revision. A 2026 concept paper for revision is not a current final requirement.
- For sterile products, use the current Annex 1 in addition to the finished-dosage-form guideline. Aseptic-processing durations and relevant holding times require defined and validated maximum times; product composition, storage method, bioburden/endotoxin risks, filtration contact time, and filling duration can become critical.

## EAEU

### Keep legal status layers separate

For EAEU work, do not substitute EU or PIC/S sources for EAEU law merely because the technical content is harmonized. Use:

1. EEC Council Decision No. 77 of 3 November 2016 for EAEU GMP.
2. EEC Board Decision No. 69 of 10 May 2018, as currently amended, for stability-study requirements.
3. EEC Board Recommendation No. 3 of 29 January 2019 for the finished-dosage-form manufacturing guideline and registration-dossier expectations.
4. EEC Council Decision No. 78 and current EAEU variation/registration rules for filing implications.
5. Applicable Member State rules only when they add a national implementation layer.

### Recommendation No. 3 of 2019

The EAEU finished-dosage-form recommendation contains a hold-time section that is technically aligned with the EMA guideline. Its key route includes:

- Intermediate hold-time validation is a GMP matter and is not normally submitted routinely in the registration application.
- Product-specific data can be required, including for sterile and biological products.
- State the need for pre-final-packaging storage and its temperature, humidity, and other environmental conditions.
- Where relevant, state, justify, and data-support the maximum bulk hold time or maximum batch manufacturing time.
- Minimise time limits and justify prolonged holds.
- As a general rule in the Recommendation, prolonged storage is >30 days for solid oral dosage forms and >24 h for sterile medicinal products.
- Where relevant, hold-time stability support is expected from at least two pilot-scale batches at representative temperature and relative humidity.
- For inter-site transport, describe and justify the route/conditions, apply EAEU GDP and EAEU GMP Annex 15 principles, and address excursions with stability evidence where necessary.
- Justify the suitability of the bulk/intermediate container-closure system.

**Status caution:** Recommendation No. 3 is an EEC Board Recommendation. Do not describe its 30-day/24-hour statements as if they were clauses of Decision No. 77 itself.

### Decision No. 69 stability interface

Use current Decision No. 69 to establish the EAEU stability framework and storage-condition evidence. When a hold or bulk storage period is long enough to create stability uncertainty, connect the hold-time justification to applicable finished-product/API stability principles and current consolidated amendments. Verify the exact clause and current consolidation before quoting it in a controlled document.

### EAEU GMP interface

Under Decision No. 77, use Part I controls for finished-dosage intermediates/bulk products and Part II for API intermediates. Control storage conditions, specifications at critical stages, processing instructions, records, deviations, validation, and transport as applicable. Do not import a fixed EU/PIC/S/China batch count into EAEU GMP unless the applicable EAEU source requires it.

## PIC/S

### Current version and version trap

At the 2026-09-24 checkpoint, the current PIC/S GMP Guide is **PE 009-18**, effective 24 September 2026. PE 009-18 revises Annex 19 and does not alter the hold-time provisions in Part I, Part II, Annex 1, Annex 9 or Annex 15 used in this module. PIC/S has published PI 006-4 on qualification and validation, but PI 006-4 enters into force on 2026-10-01 and was therefore not yet effective at the September checkpoint. **Update 2026-10-08:** PI 006-4 is now effective and supersedes PI 006-3; read [pics-pi006-4-validation-map.md](pics-pi006-4-validation-map.md). The joint EU-PIC/S Annex 15 revision remains a concept/revision process; do not cite draft text as current Annex 15.

### Finished-dosage-form intermediates and bulk products

PE 009-18 Part I Chapter 5 provides the general GMP basis:

- handling, storage, processing, and packaging should follow written procedures/instructions and be recorded where necessary;
- intermediate and bulk products purchased as such are handled as starting materials on receipt;
- all materials and products are stored under appropriate conditions established by the manufacturer;
- product must be protected from microbial and other contamination at each processing stage.

Part I does not establish a universal 30-day limit for intermediate/bulk storage. Set and justify the maximum time based on product/process knowledge, QRM, validation, stability, microbiological risk, container/closure, and registered commitments.

Use Annex 15 for validation strategy, approved protocols, QRM, process validation, transport verification, change control, and lifecycle review. For liquids, creams and ointments, PE 009-18 Annex 9 adds a dosage-form-specific control: when the finished product is not immediately packaged, the maximum storage period and storage conditions should be specified and respected. Do not generalize Annex 9 to unrelated dosage forms without stating the scope.

### Sterile products under Annex 1

PIC/S PE 009-18 Annex 1 requires aseptic preparation/processing durations to be minimised and limited to defined and validated maximum times. Relevant limits include, as applicable:

- time between cleaning/drying and sterilisation of equipment/components/containers;
- sterile hold time before use;
- time from product preparation to sterilisation or sterilising filtration through end of filling;
- sterile product hold before filling;
- aseptic processing time and filling time;
- pre-filtration fluid hold and effects on bioburden;
- maximum filtration/contact time.

These sterile controls are more product- and process-specific than a generic 24-hour or 30-day rule.

### API intermediates under Part II

PIC/S PE 009-18 Part II, reflecting ICH Q7, requires:

- master production instructions to include time limits for individual steps and/or total process where appropriate;
- storage instructions for intermediates/APIs, including special conditions and time limits where appropriate;
- specified time limits to be met, with deviations documented and evaluated;
- intermediates held for further processing to be stored under appropriate conditions to ensure suitability for use;
- appropriate storage/transport controls and records where conditions are critical.

Do not apply Part II definitions and API-intermediate rules to finished-dosage-form intermediates without stating the scope change.

## United States FDA

### Binding CGMP baseline

21 CFR 211.111 is the core binding requirement for finished pharmaceuticals: when appropriate, establish time limits for completion of each phase of production to assure drug-product quality. A deviation may be acceptable only if it does not compromise quality and is justified and documented.

Read with, as applicable:

- 21 CFR 211.100 for written production/process-control procedures;
- 21 CFR 211.110 for in-process controls;
- 21 CFR 211.113 for microbiological contamination controls;
- 21 CFR 211.188 for batch production/control records;
- 21 CFR 211.192 for investigation of unexplained discrepancies/failures;
- approved NDA/ANDA/BLA manufacturing commitments and post-approval change requirements.

There is no universal 30-day FDA CGMP threshold for finished-dosage intermediates.

### FDA CGMP Q&A for in-process/intermediate holds

FDA's CGMP Q&A states that, for chemically and physically stable in-process/intermediate materials, a risk- and science-based assessment can establish a holding period without a separate formal stability study when it provides adequate assurance of suitability. The assessment can include sampling/testing at a risk-selected stage.

For unstable materials, or materials held longer than the period established by the risk assessment, use a formal stability study under an approved protocol. FDA also recommends evaluating the held intermediate through the point of use and, for this case, long-term monitoring of finished-product batches manufactured from the held intermediate.

Treat the Q&A as nonbinding FDA current thinking, not as a CFR requirement.

### Sterile products

FDA's final aseptic-processing guidance interprets 21 CFR 211.111 for sterile manufacture. Establish data-supported time limits, where appropriate, for phases such as bulk compounding to sterilisation, filtration, product exposure on the line, and storage of sterilised equipment/components/closures. Consider bioburden and endotoxin when setting time limits.

### API intermediates

For API intermediates, use FDA's ICH Q7 guidance. It expects time limits in master production instructions where appropriate, storage instructions including time limits where appropriate, documentation/evaluation of deviations from specified limits, and appropriate storage of intermediates held for further processing. This is guidance, not the same legal basis as 21 CFR Part 211 for finished pharmaceuticals.

## WHO and emerging ICH Q1 interface

WHO TRS 992 Annex 4, *General guidance on hold-time studies* (2015), is a useful cross-jurisdiction technical reference focused mainly on non-sterile solid dosage-form manufacture, with principles also relevant to some other dosage forms. It states that maximum acceptable holding periods should be established and supported by relevant data, and that intermediates/bulk should normally not be stored beyond the established hold time. Treat it as WHO technical guidance, not as a substitute for the applicable national/regional requirement.

A consolidated ICH Q1 guideline was issued at Step 2b and, in the US, FDA published it as **draft guidance in June 2025 — not for implementation**. Its Section 9 contains detailed emerging concepts for processing and holding times, including representative temperature/humidity, transport between sites, maximum established holds, cumulative hold-time assessment in process validation and dedicated cumulative-hold studies where stability risk exists. It also uses examples of prolonged drug-product-intermediate holds such as more than 30 days for solid dosage forms and more than 24 hours for non-solid/sterile products.

Do not present the Step 2b text as a current binding ICH/FDA/EU/EAEU/PIC/S requirement. Track final ICH adoption and each jurisdiction's implementation before changing controlled company procedures solely on this draft.

## Study-design regulatory synthesis

Use the following sequence. Label the resulting elements as regulatory requirement, source-specific guidance, or professional assessment.

### 1. Define the decision and the material

Document:

- material/stage and intended downstream use;
- exact start and stop event for the hold clock;
- proposed maximum hold and routine operating hold;
- whether the hold is one continuous interval or cumulative across multiple intervals;
- product markets and dossier commitments.

### 2. Assess risk before selecting study extent

At minimum consider:

- chemical degradation and impurity formation;
- assay/potency loss;
- moisture gain/loss and water activity where relevant;
- physical changes: particle size/distribution, flow, density, hardness/friability, polymorph, viscosity, phase separation, precipitation;
- blend uniformity or segregation;
- dissolution/release performance;
- microbial proliferation, bioburden and endotoxin where relevant;
- oxygen/light/headspace sensitivity;
- contamination, cross-contamination, mix-up and container integrity;
- temperature/RH excursions;
- transport vibration/time where inter-site movement occurs;
- effect on downstream processing and finished-product CQAs;
- cumulative exposure across repeated holds.

### 3. Select study batches and scale from the governing source

Do not use one global default number of batches.

- China oral-solid CDE guide: at least pilot scale and at least one batch; commercial confirmation if study is below commercial scale; risk can justify additional batches/strengths.
- EU finished-dosage-form guideline: where relevant, at least two pilot-scale batches for stability support of prolonged bulk holds; current EU variation Q.II.b.3(e) requires a minimum of two pilot- or commercial-scale batches for the proposed change route.
- EAEU Recommendation No. 3: where relevant, at least two pilot-scale batches for hold-time stability support.
- PIC/S PE 009-18: no general fixed batch count for finished-dosage hold-time studies; justify validation extent using QRM and process knowledge. API Part II likewise does not create a universal finished-dose batch number.
- FDA: no fixed batch count in 21 CFR 211.111; justify the evidence package scientifically. A formal stability protocol is expected by FDA Q&A for unstable materials or holds beyond the risk-assessed period.

### 4. Make the proposed maximum hold the study challenge

The study should cover the intended maximum hold. If operational variability can exceed the nominal target, define whether a justified study margin, worst-case exposure, or separate deviation strategy is needed. Do not invent a mandatory percentage overage such as `maximum hold + 20%` unless an applicable source or company standard expressly requires it.

### 5. Use representative storage and container conditions

Match commercial temporary-storage conditions as closely as possible. Address:

- temperature/RH and monitoring;
- container material, closure, liner and seal;
- fill level/headspace and worst case for oxygen/moisture exposure;
- light protection;
- agitation or settling where relevant;
- transport/transfer if part of the actual process.

### 6. Select meaningful time points and tests

Do not impose a universal time-point scheme across markets.

- China oral-solid guide: at least start, intermediate, and end points.
- EU/EAEU/FDA/PIC/S: use sufficient time points to characterize the relevant trend and support the decision; product-specific guidance can add requirements.

Choose attributes linked to the identified risks and downstream/final-product CQAs. Passing a final intermediate specification alone is not always sufficient if the hold can alter downstream processability or final-product performance.

### 7. Define decision rules before execution

Predefine:

- study objective and proposed maximum hold;
- acceptance criteria and trend evaluation;
- treatment of OOS/OOT/atypical results and missing data;
- handling of excursions;
- finished-product linkage if intermediate changes are observed;
- criteria for shortening, confirming, or extending the hold time;
- commercial-scale confirmation commitment where applicable.

Do not retrospectively loosen acceptance criteria to preserve the proposed hold time.

## Routine GMP controls after the hold time is established

A compliant operating system should normally include:

- approved hold-time limits and storage conditions in the relevant manufacturing instruction/SOP or linked controlled record;
- unambiguous clock start/stop events;
- actual date/time recording for each relevant batch;
- status/identity and container/closure controls;
- environmental monitoring or storage-condition records where critical;
- escalation before or at the approved maximum time;
- deviation and scientific impact assessment for an exceeded hold time;
- no automatic release by retest alone after an exceeded limit unless a justified, approved investigation supports disposition under the governing system;
- change control and regulatory assessment before changing an approved/registered hold time or storage condition;
- periodic trend review/PQR or equivalent linkage where hold-related signals are relevant.

## Change-control triggers requiring reassessment

Reassess or reconfirm hold-time evidence when relevant changes affect the prior study basis, including:

- formulation or concentration;
- manufacturing process or sequence;
- equipment type/scale or transfer mechanism;
- site/room/environmental controls;
- batch size or subdivision strategy;
- temporary container/liner/closure or fill level;
- storage temperature/RH/light condition;
- transport route or duration;
- analytical method or specification relevant to the hold;
- known degradation/microbial trend or repeated hold-time deviations;
- extension of the maximum hold or cumulative manufacturing time.

The regulatory filing category must be assessed by market; a scientifically acceptable hold-time extension is not automatically a reportable or non-reportable change in every jurisdiction.

## Inspection and audit checklist

For an intermediate/bulk hold-time review, confirm:

1. scope and terminology match the actual material/product type;
2. each hold point has a defined clock start and stop event;
3. risk assessment identifies both material quality and downstream/final-product impact;
4. proposed maximum hold is supported by representative data;
5. batch number and scale are justified by the applicable jurisdiction, not copied from another market;
6. storage container/closure and environmental conditions are representative;
7. study time points and attributes are scientifically appropriate;
8. microbiological risk is addressed where relevant;
9. cross-site transport and excursions are included when part of routine manufacture;
10. the batch record captures actual elapsed times;
11. exceeded holds enter deviation/impact-assessment controls rather than automatic retesting into compliance;
12. change control and regulatory assessment cover hold-time/storage-condition changes;
13. finished-product stability or performance is linked when intermediate trends could propagate downstream;
14. current registration/variation commitments are consistent across markets and sites;
15. cleaning hold times, sterilised-equipment hold times, and product/intermediate hold times are not conflated.

## Red flags

Escalate or correct the following:

- stating that `30 days is the global maximum hold time`;
- treating China CDE 2025 No. 6 as universal for all dosage forms;
- applying EU/EAEU `prolonged storage >30 days` as a PIC/S or FDA legal threshold;
- extending a hold solely because the end-point sample passes release testing;
- using one batch count globally without source-specific justification;
- ignoring microbial/endotoxin risk for aqueous or sterile-process intermediates;
- studying a laboratory container while routine production uses a materially different bulk container without justification;
- excluding transport time from the hold model when product moves between sites;
- failing to define clock start/stop points in batch records;
- repeated exceeded holds managed as routine exceptions rather than trend/change/CAPA signals;
- using a draft Annex 15 revision or PI 006-4 before its effective date as a current requirement;
- confusing product/intermediate hold time with cleaning hold time or equipment sterile-hold time.
